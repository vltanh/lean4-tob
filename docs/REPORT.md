# Audit of the papers and the formalization

This report has what the five papers have in common. The audit of each paper is in a report of its own: [ECToBlob](reports/ECToBlob.md), [TOB-QMC](reports/TOBQMC.md), [TINNiK](reports/TINNiK.md), [Allman et al. 2023](reports/AllmanEtAl2023.md), [Gusfield et al. 2007](reports/Gusfield2007.md), the last with the sources that only Gusfield et al. use. Each has the paper's errors and gaps, missing and redundant hypotheses, readings, departures from its proofs, the dependencies of its results and what isn't formalized, under the numbers of Sections 3 to 9 here, which point to them.

## 1. Summary

- **Errors.** ECToBlob's (3.3) overcounts (E2), and its Remark 8.4, worded as what the argument "indicates", fails on a non-binary network, by an explicit tree and `T₀` (E35). Its Remark 3.3 cites for "no anomalous quartets" a paper whose definition is non-strict, under which (3.1) and the conclusion of Theorem 3.1 fail on a null set of parameters (E36); the paper's own use is strict. Line 1065 on uniform p-values is false under the displayed tree model (E37). The claims of TOB-QMC's supplement that its Lemma 7 and Theorem 1 hold at
  arbitrary level are false at level 2 (E25), its Lemma 2's converse is false (E23), and so is its claim that positive edge
  lengths exclude the centroid (E28). TINNiK has a misprint in the count of quartets sharing three taxa (E29), and its cut model is an open set on which the MLE need not exist at some counts with a zero (E31); neither affects a result. Gusfield et al. 2007: Theorem 4 is false as printed, because the definition of "efficient" omits the node where a blob is entered (E42) and because it doesn't exclude the site-free arcs that line 97 allows (E43); it holds once these are repaired, up to two conventions (E44). The first repair is in print: Gusfield's 2014 book *ReCombinatorics* counts that node, in a stricter definition, and states the theorem with it, though in the snippets we could read it doesn't say that the 2007 definition fails (E42). Theorem 10 is false if `R(M + S)` is read by line 385's definition: for single crossovers by a counterexample in Lean, and for multiple crossovers given one search that the kernel hasn't completed. The paper's own use of the symbol at lines 525 and 560 suggests a slip for `R_S(M)`, and with that it is true and proved (E51). Also false as printed: the characterization of the most tree-like network at line 373 (E45, E67), line 554 on galled networks, probably `M` for `M + S` as at line 552 (E49), line 560 on visible nodes, which needs distinct columns (E50), the step at line 403 on one `M` (E48), and line 180's wording of "reduced" (E47). None of these touches Theorem 1 or the refutation of the conjectures. Allman et al. 2023: a side remark in the proof of Proposition 1 is false for some `ε` (E57). No numbered result of that paper fails, reading a subnetwork as connected; read literally, Lemma 2 does (E55).
- **Gaps.** The proof of ECToBlob's Theorem 8.2 has the most serious ones: the quartet witnessing a false edge may have a
  trivial 4-blob, where the T1 null holds (E12); this needed a new 1,100-line argument, [`ECToBlob.falseEdge_witness`](../src/ECToBlob/FalseEdge.lean#L807), and
  changes no statement. Others: E1, E3, E4, E6, E7, E13–E17 (ECToBlob); E18–E22, E24, E39 (TOB-QMC); E30, E32, E33, E40 (TINNiK); E52, E53 (Gusfield et al. 2007); E56, E58–E62 (Allman et al. 2023). All are repaired. Only E22 changes a statement among these, the margin of supplement Lemma 4. Under the displayed tree model on any network, Theorem 8.2 holds for Bon and BBC and fails for Cauchy and CBC, under the extension of E7 (E37).
- **Missing hypotheses.** ECToBlob's Theorem 8.2 needs generic parameters for the literal general model (E10, agreed) and, for its Cauchy and CBC variants, positive alternatives (E11, agreed; E11 holds for Lean's continuous extension of the Cauchy transform at `p = 1`, where the printed formula is undefined). Lemma 2 of Allman et al. 2023 needs `M` connected (E55). Gusfield et al. 2007: see E42–E51, E67. Section 4 has all of them.
- **Implicit conventions, notation slips, loose sentences and misprints.** E44, conventions of Gusfield et al. 2007; E43 and E46, a case line 97 allows and the statements must exclude; E54 and E66, misprints in the two PMC manuscripts; E57, E63, E64, E65, smaller points in Allman et al. 2023, where E64 is an unstated qualification, "internal", on edges between trivial blobs; E13, E68–E74, loose sentences of ECToBlob and TOB-QMC, most on running times, none affecting a result; E75, what TINNiK's "straightforward" on missing taxa needs; E34, the older ones.
- **Redundant hypotheses.** Section 5. The most notable: the disjointness of `A₁, A₂` and of `B₁, B₂` in Theorem 4.1 of
  ECToBlob; all the refinement hypotheses in TOB-QMC's Lemma 4 except [`TOBQMC.RefinesBlob`](../src/TOBQMC/Level1.lean#L40); `0 ≤ δ` in TINNiK's Theorem 4; the binary hypothesis in Allman et al.'s Theorem 2 and Lemma 3.
- **Use of cited results.** TINNiK's Theorem 2 is applied beyond its printed hypothesis (b) (E30), TOB-QMC cites the statement
  of TINNiK's Theorem 4 for what is a step of its proof (E21), and ECToBlob's Proposition 5.1 adapts a theorem by "checking
  the argument", which turns out right (E5). Gusfield et al. 2007 restate the definition of a reduced galled tree too weakly (E47), and apply Theorem 4 where it doesn't hold (line 418, E53). Section 2 lists gaps in the proofs of cited results. Among results only cited, Theorem 36 of NANUQ is false as printed, at the level of the distance, and its proof picks the wrong level; Studier and Keppler's Lemma fails at polytomies, a failure of wording if the letter means binary trees, and their theorem holds; one direction of Corollary 2.1 of Gusfield et al. 2004 is false, though that source's theorems hold (E76). Section 3 groups the points on cited works in E76–E85.

### Gaps in the proofs of cited results

Proving the cited results by their sources' arguments exposed these. All the results hold, except NANUQ's Theorem 36 as printed, Studier and Keppler's Lemma at polytomies, if it isn't meant for binary trees only, and one direction of Corollary 2.1 of Gusfield et al. 2004 (E76). The gaps of Allman et al. 2023 are E55–E66, since it is a primary paper. E76–E85 in Section 3 are the later points on cited works.

- **Gusfield, Eddhu, Langley 2004.** See E76, E77.
- **Myers, Griffiths 2003.** Their equation (4) counts all segregating sites. The form with distinct columns, which Gusfield et al. state at line 559, comes from their remark that the bound applies to any subset of sites. Their treatment is for single crossovers; line 559's "the same proof establishes" the bound for multiple crossovers is right ([`MyersGriffiths2003.haplotypeBound_le_R`](../src/External/MyersGriffiths2003/Bound.lean#L72)). See also E78.
- **NANUQ (Allman, Baños, Rhodes 2019), Theorem 36.** False as printed at the level of the distance: line 2023 of our text says binary networks have `p_i ≠ 1/3`, but Definition 8 calls the centroid tree-like, and a binary level-1 network with a `3₂`-cycle has CF `(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)` at positive lengths ([`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.ThreeTwo.centroid_N₃`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/ThreeTwoCycle.lean#L1024)). There the star test rejects with probability tending to `β` only, so for no levels does the distance reach the theoretical one with probability tending to 1 ([`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.not_tendsto_of_centroid`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/NANUQ.lean#L1120), [`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.ThreeTwo.not_theorem36AsPrinted_N₃`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/ThreeTwoCycle.lean#L1089)). The formal statement [`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.Theorem36AsPrinted`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/NANUQ.lean#L1454) has the printed hypothesis and quantifiers, at `ρ = 0`, and concludes at the distance, which is the reduction its proof makes at the start, not the printed conclusion on the network inferred. On `N₃` that conclusion fails too, by an argument by hand, not in Lean: on the star class the distance is constant, and its circular decomposition has no split `ab|cd`. The theorem holds with no centroid CF, which holds off a null set ([`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.theorem36_generic`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/Proposition9.lean#L1664)). Its proof says "we choose `α_m` to be the minimum of the `α'_m`", which must be the maximum, since a test rejects when the p-value is below the level ([`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.exists_levels`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/NANUQ.lean#L660)); and `α'_m` is not a number, since the law of the test depends on the estimated `φ̂`. Steps 4 and 5, to the splits graph, are not formalized.
- **Studier and Keppler 1988, neighbor joining (a failure of wording, if the letter means binary trees).** Assuming "a tree in which all branches are positive", page 730 attributes to Saitou and Nei the Lemma "If i and j are neighbors, then S_ij is the strictly least element in its row and column", with the proof "Each summand in formula (5a) is positive". It fails at a polytomy: on a 4-star with unit weights all `S_ij` are equal ([`External.StudierKeppler1988.not_lemmaAsPrinted`](../src/External/StudierKeppler1988/Lemma.lean#L95)). The letter never defines "neighbors", read here as a cherry of a resolution, and its Figure 1 is binary. If it means binary trees, the star is outside its scope. Their theorem holds, with polytomies too: [`Phylogenetics.NJ.exists_QC_lt_of_not_nbr`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/NeighborJoining.lean#L785), by a different comparison (Section 7).
- **Ané et al. 2024, Definition 2.** It calls a class-1 quartet anomalous when `P(T₁|N) < 1/3`, so that a CF at `1/3` is not anomalous, while TINNiK and TOB-QMC count it as anomalous. Consequences in E36.
- **Mirarab et al. 2014, ASTRAL.** As extracted, the recursion shows only `A' ∈ X`, while the prose requires `A - A' ∈ X` too; Lean requires both. This may be an artifact of extraction. The sources were read through a summarizing fetch, and the supplement with the proof of their Theorem 1 was not accessible, so [`Phylogenetics.IsTraceback.isAstralOptimal`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/AstralDP.lean#L1103) is not compared with that proof.

### Before the paper

- Gusfield, Bansal, Bafna, Song, [J. Comput. Biol. 14 (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1089/cmb.2006.0137): a decomposition theorem for phylogenetic networks that exposes "a maximal embedded tree structure". TOB-QMC cites it for the tree of blobs. It is a primary paper here: see Section 3.
- Solís-Lemus, Ané, [PLoS Genet. 12 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1005896) (SNaQ, pseudolikelihood on quartets); Baños, [Bull. Math. Biol. 81 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-018-0485-4) ([arXiv 2017-11-28](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10545); read for its Proposition 7): "every cycle of size at least 4 and every hybrid node in a cycle of size at least 5" of a level-1 network are identifiable from gene tree quartets; Allman, Baños, Rhodes, [NANUQ, Algorithms Mol. Biol. 14 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13015-019-0159-2) ([arXiv 2019-05-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07050)): level-1 inference from quartet hypothesis tests and a quartet intertaxon distance. TINNiK's introduction sets its algorithm against these level-1 methods.
- Allman, Baños, Mitchell, Rhodes, [J. Math. Biol. 86 (2023), 10](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-022-01838-9) ([arXiv 2022-05-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03466)): "most features of the unrooted tree of blobs" are determined by the distribution of gene quartet topologies, with no assumption on the level, through a B-quartet inference rule. It names the difficulty the three papers meet in different ways: a B-quartet of the network can have a cut-edge split on its induced 4-taxon network, "the main obstacle". It is a primary paper here: see Section 3.
- Xu, Ané, [J. Math. Biol. 86 (2023), 12](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-022-01847-8) ([arXiv 2021-10-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11814)): the "distance split tree" built from average pairwise distances refines the tree of blobs, and at level 1 equals the tree of blobs refined to separate polytomies from blobs (Theorem 10). Its Conjecture 9 is taken up by a preprint under Since the paper. ECToBlob's §10 suggests applying its framework to distances.
- Solís-Lemus, Yang, Ané, [Syst. Biol. 65 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syw030): species tree methods can be inconsistent under gene flow. Dinh, Baños, [Mol. Biol. Evol. 42 (2025), msaf049](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaf049) (published 2025-03-07): under the NMSC, "including nonanomalous scenarios", ASTRAL can produce a tree "that does not correspond to any topology displayed by the true underlying network". ECToBlob cites both as the reason a statement about quartet optima on networks is needed (its [14, 37]); TOB-QMC cites Dinh and Baños, with Molloy, Durvasula, Sankararaman 2021 (its [12], [27]).
- Ané, Fogg, Allman, Baños, Rhodes, [J. Math. Biol. 88 (2024), 29](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-024-02050-7) ([bioRxiv 2023-08-21](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.08.18.553582)): under common inheritance "4-taxon networks are never anomalous"; under independent and correlated inheritance simulations find "truly anomalous networks are rare" and many networks close to the anomaly zone. Source of the no-anomaly hypotheses of TOB-QMC and ECToBlob, and of the exchangeability of Section 2.
- Rhodes, Baños, Xu, Ané, [Adv. Appl. Math. 163 (2025), 102804](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2024.102804) ([arXiv 2024-02-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11693)): circular orders of outer-labeled planar blobs are identified from 4-taxon subnetworks. Its Corollary 6.6 gives the tree of blobs under the displayed tree model, common inheritance, and independent inheritance with no anomalous quartets. Its Lemma A.1 classifies 4-taxon networks, and ECToBlob says of its Corollary 4.2 that it extends part of that lemma to more taxa.
- Huber, van Iersel, Jones, Moulton, Veenema-Nipius, [Bull. Math. Biol. 87 (2025), 136](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-025-01510-5) ([arXiv 2024-08-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12997)). Theorem 5.1, for binary semi-directed networks, which "does not put any restriction on the level": a nontrivial `A|B` is a cut-edge split exactly when `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` is a cut-edge split of the quarnet for all `a₁, a₂ ∈ A`, `b₁, b₂ ∈ B`. Corollary 5.6: the quarnets determine the blob tree. TOB-QMC's Lemma 2 is the forward direction, as TOB-QMC says.
- Frohn, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, Jones, Kelk, [J. Comput. Syst. Sci. 152 (2025), 103655](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcss.2025.103655) ([arXiv 2024-09-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06034)). Lemma 1 sharpens Huber et al.'s theorem by fixing `a₁` and `b₁`. Theorem 5 reconstructs the tree of blobs of a binary network of any level in `O(n³)` time from `O(n³)` quarnet-splits, and Proposition 6 gives a lower bound of `Ω(n log n + ℓn)` quarnet-splits at level `ℓ`. For binary networks ECToBlob's Corollary 4.2 is this Lemma 1, as ECToBlob says: it "follows from Lemma 1 of [17], which depends on results in [22]. Our proof is considerably briefer and we relax the binary condition". ECToBlob's Theorem 4.1 goes further in two ways: the network need not be binary, and the partitions `A₁, A₂` and `B₁, B₂` are arbitrary.

Huber et al. and Frohn et al. work from exact quarnet information, with no statistical model. Both appeared after the TINNiK preprint and do not build on its algorithm.

What the papers add. TINNiK ([bioRxiv 2024-04-24](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.04.20.590418)) turns the sketch of Allman et al. 2023 into an algorithm with a consistency theorem (Theorem 4) and writes "We know of no other proposed algorithm for tree of blobs inference from biological data"; ECToBlob and TOB-QMC both describe it as the first. TOB-QMC and ECToBlob add a different outline: an optimum of the quartet criterion refines the tree of blobs, and edges are then contracted by quartet tests. We found no earlier statement of that refinement result, and no earlier proof of TINNiK's Theorem 4. Section 2 lists the cited results the proofs use, including Mitchell et al. 2019 and Rhodes 2020.

### Concurrent work

- TOB-QMC and ECToBlob. First public versions: TOB-QMC [bioRxiv v1, 2025-11-06](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.05.686850), under the title "Fast and accurate tree of blobs reconstruction under the network multispecies coalescent"; ECToBlob [bioRxiv v1, 2026-05-31](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.28.728501). ECToBlob's statement: "When this work was well underway, we learned a different group was independently pursuing the same outline for tree of blobs inference", with its research dated to a residence at ICERM in Fall 2024, and thanks to Dai, Han and Molloy "for sharing their pre-publication work". TOB-QMC v5 does not mention ECToBlob; it thanks Ané, Allman, Baños and Rhodes for feedback.
  - Same result: an optimum of the quartet criterion refines the tree of blobs. TOB-QMC's Theorem 1 assumes the binary NMSC model and T-quartet-nonanomalous networks. ECToBlob's Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.6 assume only two conditions on the gene tree distribution, which its Remark 3.3 says hold for the NMSC with no anomalous quartets, for common inheritance and for the displayed tree model.
  - Same question by another method: the contraction step. TOB-QMC applies TINNiK's tests (ECToBlob compares with the T3 option) to `O(n)` four-taxon subsets around an edge and keeps the smallest p-value, with guarantees at level 1 (its Corollary 1, Theorem 2). ECToBlob applies the T1 test to the bipartition, quadripartition or multipartition sets of an edge, combines p-values under dependence, and contracts one step at a time; its Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 8.2 hold at any level. ECToBlob's §10 gives the authors' comparison of the two.
- Holtgrefe, Huber, van Iersel, Jones, Martin, Moulton, Squirrel, [Mol. Biol. Evol. 42 (2025), msaf067](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaf067) ([bioRxiv 2024-11-03](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.11.01.621567); abstract only): level-1 networks from quarnets or alignments, "combinatorially consistent" on full triangle-free quarnet sets. TOB-QMC says it was released during its study and "creates candidate TOBs, through edge contractions, which is similar to TOB-QMC", from sequence data and with no statistical guarantee under the NMSC.
- Englander, Frohn, Gross, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, Jones, Sullivant, [bioRxiv 2025-04-24](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.18.649493) (v4, 2026-07-04; preprint, no journal link in the bioRxiv API; abstract only): claims that under the Jukes-Cantor and Kimura 2-parameter models any two binary semi-directed networks displaying different quartets are distinguishable, so that the tree of blobs is identifiable at any level. Same question on sequence data. ECToBlob cites it.

Treelikeness beyond level 1. TOB-QMC's oracle asks only whether the network induced on four taxa has a 4-blob. No work we read proves or refutes that this suffices at higher level, on all four-taxon subsets around an edge.

- TOB-QMC v5 proves it at level 1 (Corollary 1, Theorem 2), and from level-2 simulations suggests "a practical heuristic for higher-level networks". Its supplement of February 26, 2026 claims it for disjoint covers at any level (its Lemma 7, Theorem 1); that claim is false at level 2 (E25).
- ECToBlob's §10: "we know of no proof of a stronger result that would justify using the T3 test. Thus the formal justification for TOB-QMC is currently limited to level-1 networks [13, Theorem 2], although simulations suggest it may perform well beyond that case."
- Huber et al.'s Theorem 5.1, Frohn et al.'s Lemma 1 and ECToBlob's Theorem 4.1 hold at any level, but they ask whether a named split `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` is a cut-edge split of the quarnet, which is more than treelikeness: Frohn et al.'s count of a quarnet-split includes identifying "the specific split".
- Allman et al. 2023 and TINNiK work at any level and use resolved topologies with the inference rule, since "the induced network on a B-quartet may or may not have a 4-blob, and can even be a tree". They do not ask about four-taxon sets around an edge of a refinement.
- Dai and Molloy's WABI paper, "Beyond level-1" and Holtgrefe et al. do not address the question. The works read only by abstract, among them BROOQS, DIMPLE and Justison et al., do not mention it there; their texts were not read.

## 11. What's next

### Open directions

- **Treelikeness beyond level 1.** ECToBlob's §10 says of the T3 test: "we know of no proof of a stronger result that would justify using the T3 test", and that the formal justification for TOB-QMC stops at level 1; Section 10 finds no published work that settles this. The question: for a network of any level and a refinement `B` of its tree of blobs, does every edge of `B` not in the tree of blobs have a non-treelike 4-taxon set among those around it?
- **Assumption 1 under correlated inheritance.** ECToBlob's §5 says "We strongly suspect Assumption 1 also holds for the NMSC model with common or correlated inheritance". For binary networks at generic parameters, "Beyond level-1" (Proposition 5.1, Section 10) extends the arguments of Allman et al. 2023 to common inheritance, `ρ = 1`, for tree of blobs identifiability, and leaves other `ρ` aside. So what is open is `0 < ρ < 1`, and `ρ = 1` on non-binary networks with ECToBlob's measure. [`AllmanEtAl2023.cfDetectable_generic`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/CFDetectable.lean#L24) here covers only `ρ = 0`. [`Phylogenetics.geneTreeDist`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Defs.lean#L94) includes `ρ`, and the equalities of the forward clauses are proved for all `ρ` ([`Phylogenetics.NMSC.cut_alternatives`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Exchangeability.lean#L550), [`Phylogenetics.NMSC.trivial_uniform`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Exchangeability.lean#L771)); the strict inequality of (5.2) is the no-anomaly hypothesis. What is needed is the generic clause: a nonvanishing witness for each 4-taxon network, for each `ρ`.
- **Rates.** All consistency statements here are asymptotic, with no rate. The proof of Theorem 8.2 runs on bounds per test, and could run on one event per quartet (below), so a non-asymptotic statement would follow from a concentration bound on each test, with constants depending on the distance of the CF from the null. What stands in the way is the non-regularity at the centroid and the boundary (E11, E13).
- **Theorem 8.2 for Cauchy and CBC on the boundary.** E11 shows these two variants need positive alternatives on null quartets. The T1 test's own null excludes the boundary in Mitchell et al., while (5.2) does not. A test that behaves at `(1,0,0)` would remove the hypothesis, as would a finite value of the Cauchy transform at `p = 1` (E11). An assumption that excludes the boundary would too, but it would drop the Displayed Tree model from these two variants, since a quartet with a cut-edge split has CF `(1,0,0)` there.
- **Coarsening, when Remark 8.4 fails.** E35 settles the remark, and [`ECToBlob.Coarsening.limitTree`](../src/ECToBlob/Remark84.lean#L780) is what the algorithm tends to. What should the algorithm do on a non-binary network when `T₀` isn't a resolution? A test that sees the false edge in E35 would have to use quartets beyond those around it.
- **The no-anomaly assumption.** ECToBlob's §10 would like to drop it. For its T1 test this is done, as it says, by the test of the line alone ([`ECToBlob.Remarks.consistency_line_nmsc`](../src/ECToBlob/Remarks.lean#L566)); it is easier than T1, since the centroid is interior to the line, so E13 disappears. The step that remains is the resolution: without the assumption, an optimum of the quartet criterion need not refine the tree of blobs, and ECToBlob asks for an algorithm that uses the CF entry that differs from the others: "Developing an algorithm to give a resolution of the tree of blobs based on that fact would be valuable". We found no work that does this.

- **How much does full decomposition cost?** Theorem 11 says `F¹ − R¹` is unbounded for single crossovers. Line 666 says "we do not know how robust these empirical results are in other ranges of data, or to multiple-crossover recombination, or when there is a fixed ancestral sequence". We found nothing on this in the works of Section 10, and Gusfield's 2014 book, whose Section 7.4.3 and Chapter 11 are about this, should be read first.
- **[`Gusfield2007.MinDecomposableOfForallS`](../src/Gusfield2007/Conjectures.lean#L118).** Line 425's "when M is S-min decomposable for all S, then M is min-decomposable" is unproved here. [`Gusfield2007.fig6_hG_necessary`](../src/Gusfield2007/Counterexamples.lean#L252) shows why it can't be got one root at a time.
- **`T̄` and the tree of blobs.** A remark that is unverified: on paper, not in Lean, and read by no independent checker. For a binary network, `T̄` of all the splits of its displayed trees should be its tree of blobs, at any level. A split `A|B` of `T̄` is compatible with every displayed split, so every displayed tree shows `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` across it, so by [`AllmanEtAl2023.InPassing.isTreelike_iff_forall_displays`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/InPassing.lean#L513) and [`ECToBlob.theorem4_1`](../src/ECToBlob/Section4.lean#L55) (or Huber et al.'s Theorem 5.1) it is a cut-edge split. Conversely a cut-edge split is in every displayed tree and is a trivial component. A script tried it on 3,000 random binary networks with at most four reticulations; the script isn't kept in the repository. Under the displayed tree model `T̄` would then be an exact estimator. Under the coalescent, where every pair of gene-tree splits is eventually incompatible, is anything left of this?
- **Duplication and loss on networks.** ECToBlob's Remark 3.4 says "it remains an open question whether these results hold for duplication and loss models when the species phylogeny has reticulations". No source defines such a model, so [`Remark34.OpenQuestion`](../src/ECToBlob/Remark34.lean#L100) has it as a parameter, and [`Remark34.remark3_4_mixture`](../src/ECToBlob/Remark34.lean#L80) is the case of a gene family that follows one tree picked at random, where no reticulation acts on each gene copy separately.
- **Distances between blobs above level 1.** Level 1 is settled in Allman et al. 2024 (Section 10). Above it: which cut edges have identifiable lengths, and which functions of the lengths are identifiable? [`AllmanEtAl2023.InPassing.blobDistance_not_identifiable`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/BlobDistance.lean#L115) is the 4-taxon case.

### Simpler proofs

Each of these is the argument of the formal proof, so it is checked in Lean. We found none of them published, in the search of Section 10.

- **ECToBlob Theorem 4.1, in domination language.** An arc `e` induces the split `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` on a quartet exactly when `e` dominates `a₁, a₂` (every root path to them uses `e`) and `b₁, b₂` are not below it, or the reverse ([`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.hasCutSplitOn_iff_separates`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Network/Cuts.lean#L253)). So a cut edge of a quartet is a statement about each taxon separately. The paper's minimal choice of quadruple stays; the funnel step becomes the comparability that E4 finds unstated: replacing one taxon by another of its block gives comparable sets of cut arcs, by four cases on how two arcs are oriented, ordered when they dominate a common taxon. This saves the funnels, the graph `G̃₄`, Figure 2 and the "two 3-blobs" remark: about 300 lines against about 1,070. An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the paper.
- **Allman et al. Lemma 3, likewise.** Since `e₀` is a cut on `Y \ {α}` and not on `Y`, the taxon `α` is below `e₀` and not dominated by it, and an arc resolving `{a,b,c,α}` on `Y \ {d}` compares with `e₀` by [`AllmanEtAl2023.order_of_isBelow_of_dominates`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/CutOrder.lean#L61), in four cases. Components and the cycle through `A, K_ab, e₀, K_cd` do not appear. It takes about 126 lines against about 1,600 for the source's route. An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the source. Smaller changes also remain possible within the source's route: `C` as a minimal subset of all of `P₁ ∪ P₂ ∪ P₃ ∪ P₄` closing up `e₀` must leave `K_ab` at `x`, so `P₁` itself is the path from `α` to `C`; and `y` reaches `α ≠ d`, so `P₃` can run straight to `y`, with no `z`.
- **TINNiK's Theorem 4: the centroid.** The paper proves `CF ≠ (1/3,1/3,1/3)` for generic parameters by a separate analyticity argument. It follows from CF-detectability: strictly cut and non-cut CFs each have two different entries. This removes that paragraph of the proof, and also the need for a separate generic set. The formal proof of Theorem 4 takes the paper's step; [`TINNiK.tests_correct`](../src/TINNiK/Basic.lean#L506) takes the shortcut, [`TINNiK.noCentroidCF_of_cfDetectable`](../src/TINNiK/Basic.lean#L352).
- **Allman et al. Theorem 1, part (b).** Instead of one parameter with three distinct CFs, take each pair of quartet trees separately. The reduction shows that the display trees of a non-treelike 4-set show at least two different quartets, so at a display tree with long arcs one of the pair has CF near 1. Non-cut then holds generically with no mixture in `γ`. This is a sketch; the formal proof follows the source's mixture.
- **Allman et al. Theorem 1, the choice of `γ`.** One explicit point suffices: all lengths 3 and `γ = 1/3`. Each endpoint CF is within `exp(-3) < 1/4` of a vertex, so the mixture has entries near `1/3`, `2/3`, `0`, with no genericity at all. An earlier version of the formal proof did this; it now follows the source.
- **Allman et al. Theorem 1, the contradiction.** With no blobs: `e₁` and `e₂` dominate a common taxon, so they are ordered, and in four cases by direction the arc nearer the blob has `a` on the `b` side in both deletions. An earlier version of the formal proof did this.
- **Allman et al. Corollary 1.** On `N` directly: statements (a) and (b) hold for a 4-set of any network, since domination off `D` sees the four taxa alone and CFs are analytic in `N`'s coordinates. This needs neither the induced network, nor the map of parameters, nor the identity of CFs. An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the source, through [`AllmanEtAl2023.induced`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/InducedNetwork.lean#L296).
- **Allman et al. Theorem 2.** Take a side `A` of the tree of blobs with two taxa of the set: the two B-quartets constrain `A` on `a, b, c, d, e`, and 32 cases leave only `A` separating `a, e` from `b, c, d`, which is the contradiction. No node of the tree of blobs is needed. An earlier version of the formal proof did this.
- **ECToBlob Theorem 8.2, one event.** One event per quartet, Lemma 8.1 applied once to all null quartets, a joint tightness bound at the centroid and growth under the alternative give one `δ` and `c₀` that make every combined test on every possible tree come out right together. So E16 disappears, with no count `K` and no bound `C(s,4)`. And [`ECToBlob.falseEdge_witness`](../src/ECToBlob/FalseEdge.lean#L807) alone gives a quartet in the multipartition of `T₀`, which serves all types, so Corollary 4.2 isn't needed. An earlier version of the formal proof did this; it now follows the paper.
- **ECToBlob Theorem 3.1.** Only the splice is needed of the attachments at lines 495–497: [`Phylogenetics.displays_splice_iff`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/Splice.lean#L159) gives its inner sums directly.
- **Rhodes Theorem 2.** `{x,y,u,v} ∈ Q` holds exactly when no side contains `u, v` and avoids `x, y`, so count such pairs directly and split them by node, with no case split on the shape of the quartet.
- **TOB-QMC's ASTRAL corollary.** Every binary gene tree occurs almost surely, so all binary trees are in the search space; there's no need for the probability of a bipartition.
- **TOB-QMC's supplement Lemma 8.** A lowest reticulation of the blob has only bridges below it, in any phylogenetic network.
- **The T3 test at the singularity.** Non-collapse needs no limit law: the T3 statistic is at most the T1 statistic, which is tight on the null, and the p-value is at least `½ χ²₁(10 t)`, since `sin² α₀ ≥ 1/10` on `[0,1]`.
- **NANUQ's Theorem 36.** Explicit levels prove it directly ([`ECToBlob.remark83_nanuq_fixed_beta`](../src/ECToBlob/Remark83.lean#L147)), with no levels built from the unknown `d`s. The formal [`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.theorem36`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/Proposition9.lean#L1636) keeps the source's route.
- **Neighbor joining.** The side with at most `n/2` clusters is shorter than Studier and Keppler's two-node contradiction, and it covers polytomies.
- **Remark 3.3's displayed tree model.** It is the limit of `ρ = 1` as all lengths grow ([`ECToBlob.abs_nmscCF_sub_displayedCF_le`](../src/ECToBlob/Remark33.lean#L461)), which also justifies the star convention, since a polytomy gives exactly `1/3` at all lengths.
- **TOB-QMC Lemma 3, the second observation.** Lines 489–492 restrict to one taxon per block and say a refinement's gain "would be amplified after reattaching". No restriction is needed: a maximizer resolves every 4-set in distinct blocks, since adding one split that resolves one more gains a positive CF on `X` directly, and that the result is binary then follows from the standard fact that a tree is binary exactly when it resolves every 4-set ([`Phylogenetics.isBinaryTree_of_forall_displays`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/Detach.lean#L177), [`Phylogenetics.IsBinaryTree.exists_displays`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/Splits.lean#L589)). An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the paper. Similarly, the contradiction at lines 472–486 adds nothing to the direct transformation it contains.
- **TOB-QMC Lemma 4.** Read on the rooted induced subgraph, with no wedge paths: for ⇐, a blob arc does not dominate the taxon below the reticulation, and an arc away from the blob has the whole blob below it or two taxa in distinct blocks joined outside it; for ⇒, descend from the top of the blob to an arc with exactly two taxa below it. An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the paper, through wedge paths and the bridge [`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.hasWedgeCutSplit_iff_hasCutSplitOn`](../src/Basic/Wedge.lean#L901).
- **TINNiK Proposition 6.** Computing the strip probabilities in rotated Cartesian coordinates, with Fubini in place of differentiating under the integral, and taking `G₄, G₅` as reflections of `G₃, G₂` at `-μ`, gives the same `d/dx Gᵢ` and needs no domination argument. An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the paper.
- **Allman et al. Theorem 3.** The first pass to one taxon per component isn't needed: removing a taxon or applying Lemma 3 covers every case. Checked in a scratch Lean file, outside the library.
- **Wilks-type limits for a trinomial.** The papers cite these limits; for a trinomial there is an elementary proof. One estimate, `|(1+v) log(1+v) - v - v²/2| ≤ 4|v|³` for `|v| ≤ 1/2` ([`Real.abs_entropyExcess_sub_le`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/EntropyExcess.lean#L50)), gives each likelihood ratio statistic as a sum of such terms, so each is uniformly close on bounded sets to a continuous function of the whitened frequencies, and an extended continuous mapping theorem, proved from the portmanteau theorem on open sets (`Phylogenetics.Trinomial.tendstoInDistribution_of_closeOnBalls`), finishes. This does not use van der Vaart's Theorem 16.7. At the centroid, Pearson's statistic is exactly `Z₁² + Z₂²` ([`Phylogenetics.Trinomial.pearsonS_centroid`](../src/Basic/Testing/Trinomial/Whiten.lean#L425)); the T1 test at the centroid is handled separately (E13).

- **Gusfield et al., the lower bounds of Figure 8.** The paper runs a program to show that a component needs two multiple-crossover recombinations, since one recombination node is a galled tree. A shorter argument: with one recombination node `x`, the incompatibility graph is bipartite. Colour a site on one side of the cycle by its state at `x`, and a site on the other side by the opposite: two incompatible sites then get different colours, and sites off the cycle are compatible with all. So a triangle of incompatible sites forces two recombination nodes, and each component of Figure 8 has one, as do those of [`Gusfield2007.evenRows`](../src/Gusfield2007/Examples.lean#L81). Checked on paper, and the triangles by script; the formal proof uses a verified search ([`Gusfield2007.LabelSearch.lt_of_check`](../src/Gusfield2007/LabelSearch.lean#L277)).
- **Gusfield et al., line 411.** Re-rooting at a row of an optimal tree, and Corollary 3, give that every `M` is min decomposable for recombinations that model mutations, with no median network ([`Gusfield2007.minDecomposable_pointwise`](../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L168)). Checked in Lean. It isn't the route line 411 points to, which stays to be built (below).
- **Gusfield et al., existence of `T̄`.** The Splits Equivalence Theorem applied to the character splits with the singleton splits gives `T̄` directly, with no contraction of the Perfect Phylogeny Theorem's tree. A sketch; the formal proof contracts, as line 264 suggests ([`Gusfield2007.IsPerfectPhylogeny.exists_normal`](../src/Gusfield2007/TreeContract.lean#L321)).
- **Allman et al. Theorem 1 (b), the 2-blob step and Lemma 1.** Neither is needed: a reticulation in a 2-blob passes the same deletion test as any other, since domination sees through 2-blobs, and "a single edge below `v`" follows from the `2|2` split against an unresolved `T`. Checked in Lean, in [`AllmanEtAl2023.exists_reduction_aux`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Reduction.lean#L673), which the later papers use; the numbered result [`AllmanEtAl2023.theorem1_b`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Theorem1b.lean#L716) follows the paper.
- **Allman et al., blobs through suppression.** "Determines" is equivalent to "no side of a cut edge away from the blob has two of the taxa" ([`AllmanEtAl2023.BlobsPass.determines_iff_noTwoInSide`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/BlobsPass.lean#L38)), which makes the passage of blobs through Definition 6 short. Checked in Lean.
- **Allman et al. Proposition 1, the bound on `CF_{ab|cd}`.** The bound alone has a shorter proof, by a potential on the event that the lineages diverge, with no conditional masses or species trees. An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the paper.

- **Gusfield et al. 2004, Theorem 2.2, in the form 2007 cites.** No contraction or deletion: if no recombination node breaks `{i, j}`, only three pairs occur; if one does, its parents differ at both sites, and tracing states up gives a cycle through each site's arc and both arcs into that node, and these cycles share an arc. This avoids the source's unproved claim that an arc can be deleted. It is the Lean proof, [`GusfieldEtAl2004.exists_isBlob_of_incompatible`](../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/SameBlob.lean#L124).
- **Reduced galled trees.** Lemma 2.3 and Theorems 2.4, 2.5 of the source go by the arrays `W1, W2, W3`, one root and single crossovers. A threshold on a line through the gall's nodes covers the three arrays at once, for any root and crossover model ([`GusfieldEtAl2004.exists_threshold`](../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/GalledTree.lean#L397)). It is the Lean proof.
