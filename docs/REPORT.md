## 1. Summary

- **Errors.** ECToBlob's (3.3) overcounts (E2), and its Remark 8.4, worded as what the argument "indicates", fails on a non-binary network, by an explicit tree and `T₀` (E35). Its Remark 3.3 cites for "no anomalous quartets" a paper whose definition is non-strict, under which (3.1) and the conclusion of Theorem 3.1 fail on a null set of parameters (E36); the paper's own use is strict. The claims of TOB-QMC's supplement that its Lemma 7 and Theorem 1 hold at
  arbitrary level are false at level 2 (E25), its Lemma 2's converse is false (E23), and so is its claim that positive edge
  lengths exclude the centroid (E28). TINNiK has a misprint in the count of quartets sharing three taxa (E29), and its cut model is an open set on which the MLE need not exist at counts with a zero (E31); neither affects a result.
- **Gaps.** The proof of ECToBlob's Theorem 8.2 has the most serious ones: the quartet witnessing a false edge may have a
  trivial 4-blob, where the T1 null holds (E12); this needed a new 1,100-line argument, [`ECToBlob.falseEdge_witness`](../src/ECToBlob/FalseEdge.lean#L1044), and
  changes no statement. Others: E1, E3, E4, E6, E7, E13–E17 (ECToBlob); E18–E22, E24, E39 (TOB-QMC); E30, E32, E33, E40 (TINNiK). All are repaired, except that Theorem 8.2 stays on binary gene trees, so the displayed tree model on non-binary networks is not covered (E37). Only E22 changes a statement, the margin of supplement Lemma 4.
- **Missing hypotheses.** ECToBlob's Theorem 8.2 needs generic parameters for the literal general model (E10, agreed) and, for its Cauchy and CBC variants, positive alternatives (E11, agreed; E11 holds for Lean's continuous extension of the Cauchy transform at `p = 1`, where the printed formula is undefined). Section 4.
- **Redundant hypotheses.** Section 5. The most notable: the disjointness of `A₁, A₂` and of `B₁, B₂` in Theorem 4.1 of
  ECToBlob; all the refinement hypotheses in TOB-QMC's Lemma 4 except [`RefinesBlob`](../src/TOBQMC/Level1.lean#L48); `0 ≤ δ` in TINNiK's Theorem 4.
- **Use of cited results.** TINNiK's Theorem 2 is applied beyond its printed hypothesis (b) (E30), TOB-QMC cites the statement
  of TINNiK's Theorem 4 for what is a step of its proof (E21), and ECToBlob's Proposition 5.1 adapts a theorem by "checking
  the argument", which turns out right (E5). Section 2 lists gaps in the proofs of cited results. Among results only cited, Theorem 36 of NANUQ is false as printed, at the level of the distance, and its proof picks the wrong level; Studier and Keppler's Lemma fails at polytomies, though their theorem holds.

### Gaps in the proofs of cited results

Proving the cited results by their sources' arguments exposed these. All the results hold, except NANUQ's Theorem 36 as printed, and Studier and Keppler's Lemma at polytomies.

- **Allman et al. 2023, §2.2 and Lemma 2.** "On a network 𝒩+ every subset of 3 taxa determines a blob" has no proof (`External.AllmanEtAl2023.exists_determines_three`, through [`exists_median`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/SubgraphBlobs.lean#L576)). In Lemma 2, the "subpath outside ℬ" needs `s ∉ ℬ`, since a degree-one node is alone in its blob and such a blob isn't determined by three or more labels, and the subnetwork must be connected from the blob, which Lemma 3's use satisfies: if `M` has `s₁, s₂, s₃` on separate components, `S` determines a blob of `M` vacuously while `s₁, s₂` can be a cherry in `N`.
- **Allman et al. 2023, Lemma 3.** The largest omission: "Since ℬ′ also lies in M" and "none of its edges are ancestral only to the taxon d" have no argument for `K_ab`. They don't follow from ℬ′ being a blob of `N′` alone. They hold because `ℬ′ ⊆ K_ab` and `d ∈ K_cd`: a directed path from `K_ab` to `d` must use `e₀`, whose head is above all of `K_cd`, so it can also lead to `c` (`External.AllmanEtAl2023.CutEdgeData.near_reaches`). Asserted without argument: "Thus e0 also separates a, b, from c, d" (the side containing `c, d` avoids ℬ′ and is connected); that `P₃` can be kept in `K_cd` (the source's reason gives connectivity only in `N′`); that `C` "passes through A, Kab, e0, and Kcd" after removing self-intersections; and that "the initial segment of P1 gives a path from α to C", which needs `P₁` to meet `C` after the removal. It does: take the last vertex of `P₁` on the directed path from `y` to `α` (`External.AllmanEtAl2023.CutEdgeData.Paths.exists_meet`). Not mentioned: that the directed path from `z` to `y` avoids `e₀`, which is what makes `e₀` used once.
- **Allman et al. 2023, Theorem 3.** "All edges in ℬ are retained" has no proof (`External.AllmanEtAl2023.exists_reaches_rep`). The first pass it belongs to isn't needed: removing a taxon or applying Lemma 3 covers every case.
- **Allman et al. 2023, Theorem 1.** Its contradiction argument is correct, but "Now e must be a cut edge in both N1 and N2" has no reason. The formal proof gets the next claim first, that the edges above `a` only attach in the `b`-component, from the given cut arcs `e₁`, `e₂` (`External.AllmanEtAl2023.IsLowPair.ends_mem_far`), and derives this one from it (`External.AllmanEtAl2023.IsLowPair.onlyCrosses_far_insert`). Also left implicit: that a hybrid node remains after the reductions, and how generic parameters on `𝒩+` reach the deleted networks ("first choosing the numerical parameters other than γ1, γ2"). The last is avoided by reading the deleted networks in the coordinates of `𝒩+`.
- **Allman et al. 2023, Corollary 1.** "They give rise to a generic condition" needs that an induced network of a binary network is binary ([`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.inducedNetwork_isBinary`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/InducedParams.lean#L436)), that the map of parameters sends valid parameters to valid ones ([`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.isMetric_inducedMetric`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/InducedParams.lean#L599)), and that preimages of null sets under it are null ([`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.isGenericNull_inducedMetric`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/InducedParams.lean#L609)): it is a surjective linear map, a coordinate projection on inheritance and sums along suppressed paths on lengths. It also needs the identity of the CFs of `N` and of its induced network, which the source states without proof ("by the structure of the NMSC model"): [`Phylogenetics.NMSC.nmscCF_inducedNetwork`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Induced.lean#L76). All of this is proved along the source's lines, except that the network is cut at the LSA (Section 7).
- **NANUQ (Allman, Baños, Rhodes 2019), Theorem 36.** False as printed at the level of the distance: line 2023 of our text says binary networks have `p_i ≠ 1/3`, but Definition 8 calls the centroid tree-like, and a binary level-1 network with a `3₂`-cycle has CF `(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)` at positive lengths ([`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.ThreeTwo.centroid_N₃`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/ThreeTwoCycle.lean#L1022)). There the star test rejects with probability tending to `β` only, so for no levels does the distance reach the theoretical one with probability tending to 1 ([`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.not_tendsto_of_centroid`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/NANUQ.lean#L1138), [`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.ThreeTwo.not_theorem36AsPrinted_N₃`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/ThreeTwoCycle.lean#L1087)). The formal statement [`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.Theorem36AsPrinted`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/NANUQ.lean#L1482) has the printed hypothesis and quantifiers, at `ρ = 0`, and concludes at the distance, which is the reduction its proof makes at the start, not the printed conclusion on the network inferred. On `N₃` that conclusion fails too, by an argument by hand, not in Lean: on the star class the distance is constant, and its circular decomposition has no split `ab|cd`. The theorem holds with no centroid CF, which holds off a null set ([`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.theorem36_generic`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/Proposition9.lean#L1635)). Its proof says "we choose `α_m` to be the minimum of the `α'_m`", which must be the maximum, since a test rejects when the p-value is below the level ([`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.exists_levels`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/NANUQ.lean#L667)); and `α'_m` is not a number, since the law of the test depends on the estimated `φ̂`. Steps 4 and 5, to the splits graph, are not formalized.
- **Studier and Keppler 1988, neighbor joining.** Their Lemma, read in a scan of the paper as saying that `S_ij` of neighbours is strictly least in its row and column, fails at a polytomy: on a 4-star with unit weights all `S_ij` are equal. Their theorem holds: [`Phylogenetics.NJ.exists_QC_lt_of_not_nbr`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/NeighborJoining.lean#L785), by a different comparison (Section 7).
- **Ané et al. 2024, Definition 2.** It calls a class-1 quartet anomalous when `P(T₁|N) < 1/3`, so that a CF at `1/3` is not anomalous, while TINNiK and TOB-QMC count it as anomalous. Consequences in E36.
- **Mirarab et al. 2014, ASTRAL.** As extracted, the recursion shows only `A' ∈ X`, while the prose requires `A - A' ∈ X` too; Lean requires both. This may be an artifact of extraction. The sources were read through a summarizing fetch, and the supplement with the proof of their Theorem 1 was not accessible, so [`Phylogenetics.IsTraceback.isAstralOptimal`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/AstralDP.lean#L1103) is not compared with that proof.

## 3. Errors and gaps in the papers

### ECToBlob

**E1. Remark 3.2.** "Any multifurcation in a potential maximizer can be arbitrarily resolved to increase the MQS score" is
loose when some probabilities vanish: an arbitrary resolution need not increase the score. Some resolution with positive
probability does, since the three probabilities on a quartet sum to 1. [`ECToBlob.remark3_2`](../src/ECToBlob/Section3.lean#L569) proves the remark.

**E2. Equation (3.3) is false as printed.** Summing over representative tuples counts a quartet once for each choice of
representatives of the other blocks, so a quartet in distinct blocks is overcounted by the product of the sizes of the
unused blocks. With blocks of sizes `(2,1,1,1,1)` and all cross-block CFs `1/3`, the left side is `3` and the right side `10/3` on a binary `T`
([`scripts/SourceChecks.lean`](../scripts/SourceChecks.lean)); a multifurcating species tree with one positive-length cherry realizes these CFs. This refutes
the identity, not Theorem 3.1. Repair, along the paper's route: weight each local term by the product of its four block sizes,
divide by `∏ᵢ |Xᵢ|`, and use the weighted local objective for the splicing. Lean: [`ECToBlob.equation3_3`](../src/ECToBlob/Section3.lean#L86), [`ECToBlob.localWeight`](../src/ECToBlob/Section3.lean#L187),
[`ECToBlob.theorem3_1`](../src/ECToBlob/Section3.lean#L488). The identity also needs the `Xᵢ` to cover `X`, as the paper's do. The side claims at lines 497–500 and 504–507, and lines 501–504 (a splice of maximizers maximizes `f_m`), are false for the printed, unweighted `f̃_m`: with block sizes `(4,1,1,1,1)` and abstract weights, the maximizers of `f_m` and of the unweighted `f̃_m` can differ (not shown realized by a network). All hold for the weighted objective. Lines 461–463 also use, unstated, that the quartets a tree displays determine its splits ([`Phylogenetics.subset_of_forall_displays`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/Splits.lean#L748)), and lines 504–507 that a side of a maximizer outside `T_B` refines one multifurcation ([`Phylogenetics.exists_mem_multifurcations_isBlockUnion`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/Detach.lean#L230)).

**E3. Corollary 3.6, proof.** "The probability that `f̂` and `f` have different sets of optima → 0" is false when `f` has
ties: `f(a)=f(b)=0`, `fₙ(a)=1/(n+1)`, `fₙ(b)=0` converges uniformly with different argmax sets for every `n`, and ties do
occur here. The result holds: the empirical optima lie among the expected ones, by the gap to non-maximizers, which is what
the corollary says. Lean: [`ECToBlob.corollary3_6`](../src/ECToBlob/Section3.lean#L1483).

**E4. Theorem 4.1, proof.** "The minimality of `|E|` implies that the funnel of `a₁` can only attach to `G̃₃` on the
`ã₂`-side" is elliptical: it needs the comparability of the sets `E(z, …)` of cut edges for different replacements. Going from
one funnel to all of them at once needs that funnels on opposite sides of an edge share no node, which is true and unstated. The "two
3-blobs" and "chain of 2-blobs" remarks are used only in part: the chain on `G̃₃` gives the comparability ([`ECToBlob.quartetCuts_subset_or_subset`](../src/ECToBlob/Section4Funnels.lean#L1204)), and funnels on opposite sides share no node ([`ECToBlob.Induces.attach_funnels`](../src/ECToBlob/Section4Funnels.lean#L301)).

**E5. Proposition 5.1.** Its proof rests on an adaptation of Allman et al.'s Theorem 1 stated by "checking the argument", and on atoms at hybrid length 0 that the source's measure doesn't have. First, it should be `|μ₀| → ∞`, as Appendix C.3 writes it, since anomalous strictly cut CFs have `μ₀ → -∞`; the two signs behave alike, since `z̄ ↦ -z̄` swaps the last two terms of `Λ_n`. Second, the limit is governed by `cos α₀ |μ₀|`, the distance from `(0, μ₀)` to the other two lines, and `|μ₀|` alone is not enough: `α₀ → π/2` as `φ₀ → 3/2`, and at fixed `n` there `|μ₀| → ∞` while `cos α₀ μ₀ → -√n`, so the law tends to that of `min(Z², (Z-√n)², (Z+√n)²)`. This does no harm at a positive CF, where `φ̂ → φ₀ < 3/2`. Lean proves the limit uniformly in the argument and the parameter ([`Phylogenetics.Trinomial.abs_cutTail_sub_chiSqOneTail_lt`](../src/Basic/Testing/Trinomial/Tests.lean#L168)), which is what the plug-in needs ([`Phylogenetics.Trinomial.eventually_abs_cutTail_estimate_sub_lt`](../src/Basic/Testing/Trinomial/Tests.lean#L286)); so the p-value is asymptotically uniform ([`Phylogenetics.QuartetTests.cutPValue_asymptoticallyUniform`](../src/Basic/Testing/Limits.lean#L416)) and `P(p < α_m) → 0` for any `α_m → 0` ([`Phylogenetics.QuartetTests.cutTest_null`](../src/Basic/Testing/Limits.lean#L445)). With no 4-blob among the induced networks, "the maximum of the `α'_m`" is over an empty family; any sequence tending to `0` serves, and Lean adds `P(χ²₁ > m)` ([`TINNiK.exists_levels`](../src/TINNiK/Basic.lean#L614)). The centroid step evaluates at hybridization parameters `0` or `1`, on the boundary of the parameter space, so "analyticity" must hold across it: the CFs are analytic on all the coordinates (`External.AllmanEtAl2023.isGenericNull_of_analyticOnNhd`). The result holds.

[`Phylogenetics.sum_geneTreeDist`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Basic.lean#L51) and [`Phylogenetics.geneTreeDist_support`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Basic.lean#L64) validate it: a law on binary rooted trees.
- **Sampling**: `Phylogenetics.iidProb dist n Ev` is the finite sum over samples, proved equal to the product measure ([`Phylogenetics.iidProb_eq_pi`](../src/Basic/Testing/IID.lean#L264)). TOB-QMC's almost-sure statement uses any probability space (`TOBQMC.IsGeneTreeSample`).
- **Test statistics** follow the implementation (MSCquartets, Mitchell et al.). The T1 p-value uses the plug-in `min(1, φ̂)` at all counts; small-count procedures of software are not modelled. Star: likelihood ratio by default, Pearson as `StarTest.pearson`.
- **Gene trees in ECToBlob's Theorem 8.2** are binary ([`ECToBlob.BinaryGeneTree`](../src/Basic/Defs.lean#L216)), which is what the proof and the NMSC give; ECToBlob's unrooted topological gene trees need not be. So the theorem covers the NMSC at any `ρ`, and, for Bon and BBC, the Displayed Tree model when all displayed trees are binary: on a cut split that model's CF is `(1,0,0)`, so the hypothesis `hpos` fails for Cauchy and CBC. It does not cover the Displayed Tree model on nonbinary networks (E37).
- **TOB-QMC's disjoint cover around an edge of a non-binary tree** (Definition 2) is read with the multipartition at the edge ([`Phylogenetics.multipartitionQuartets`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/Defs.lean#L107)), since Theorem 2 is stated there.

## 9. Not formalized

- **Empirical claims**: the simulations and data analyses of all three papers, and measured running times. These aren't mathematical claims.
- **Running times on a machine.** What's proved are counts of operations (Section 6): [`Phylogenetics.NJ.ops_run_le`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/NeighborJoining.lean#L2024), [`TINNiK.tinnikOperations_nj_le`](../src/TINNiK/NJ.lean#L65), [`TOBQMC.card_threeFixOneAlter_total_le`](../src/TOBQMC/Counts.lean#L240), [`TOBQMC.threeFixOneAlterOperations_le`](../src/TOBQMC/Counts.lean#L273), [`TOBQMC.search_total_le_of_limit_le`](../src/TOBQMC/Counts.lean#L401), [`ECToBlob.Counts.ectoblobOperations_le`](../src/ECToBlob/Counts.lean#L827). LCA data structures aren't formalized, nor ASTRAL's running time.
- **That a heuristic finds an optimum.** No source claims this of TREE-QMC, and nothing here does. For ASTRAL, what's provable is proved: its dynamic program is exact on its search space ([`Phylogenetics.IsTraceback.isAstralOptimal`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/AstralDP.lean#L1103), [`TOBQMC.astral_refines_treeOfBlobs`](../src/TOBQMC/Astral.lean#L159)).
- **ECToBlob's Remark 3.4**: it cites results on duplication and loss models and states an open question. No such model is in the library.
- **ECToBlob's §10, beyond what's proved**: post-selection p-values, which it calls future work; the analogue of Assumption 1 for sum-of-pairs distances, which it cites, and a test for that data type (only the four-point facts on trees are proved: [`Phylogenetics.FourPoint.sumOfPairs_of_displays`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/FourPoint.lean#L121)); and the Log-det distance it says one "might" use. Combining edge p-values under CBC should fail, by our sketch: only the combination step is in Lean ([`ECToBlob.Remarks.combineP_cbc_eq_one`](../src/ECToBlob/Remarks.lean#L804)).
- **ECToBlob's Theorem 8.2 for the displayed tree model on non-binary networks** (E37).
- **TOB-QMC's supplement Lemma 7 and Theorem 1 at arbitrary level**, which are false (E25); at level 1 they are Lemma 4 and Theorem 2.
- **TOB-QMC's implementation of the tests at lines 133–137**, the star test on the minimizer only (Section 6).

- **TINNiK's extension to gene trees with missing taxa**, and NANUQ's.

- **NANUQ beyond its distance**: NeighborNet, circular networks and splits graphs; its Theorems 15, 23, 34, Corollaries 16, 24, 31, 35, Lemmas 19, 21, 25–28, 32, 33, Proposition 30, and gene trees on subsets of taxa. ## 10. The papers in the literature

The search was made on 2026-10-31 in OpenAlex (works citing TINNiK, TOB-QMC, ECToBlob, Allman et al. 2023, Ané et al. 2024, Huber et al. 2025, "Beyond level-1", Rhodes et al. 2025, Xu and Ané 2023, NANUQ+, and the WABI paper of Dai and Molloy; keyword searches), Crossref (title searches; its `updates` filter for corrections), Europe PMC and PubMed, the bioRxiv API (versions, journal links), the arXiv API, GitHub's search API, the Archive of Formal Proofs, and web search. We kept every correction or counterexample found, and otherwise the works that state or claim a result about the tree of blobs, about which four-taxon information decides it, or about a method that starts from a tree of blobs with a stated guarantee. Empirical studies, reviews and work on other problems were screened by abstract and left out. Each statement below comes from the cited work's abstract or numbered statements, and we say where only the abstract was read. We could not read the full text of the Genome Research version of TOB-QMC (subscription), of BROOQS, DIMPLE and Justison et al. (bioRxiv answered HTTP 429), or of Rasschaert's preprint (SSRN answered HTTP 403). Citation indexes lag: OpenAlex lists one work citing ECToBlob and two citing TOB-QMC, so recent citing work may be missing. Crossref's deposited reference lists add BROOQS and Justison et al. for TOB-QMC, and Justison et al. for ECToBlob. A preprint's results are reported as claimed.

### Before the paper

- Gusfield, Bansal, Bafna, Song, [J. Comput. Biol. 14 (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1089/cmb.2006.0137): a decomposition theorem for phylogenetic networks that exposes "a maximal embedded tree structure" (abstract only). TOB-QMC cites it for the tree of blobs.
- Solís-Lemus, Ané, [PLoS Genet. 12 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1005896) (SNaQ, pseudolikelihood on quartets); Baños, [Bull. Math. Biol. 81 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-018-0485-4) ([arXiv 2017-11-28](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10545); abstract only): "every cycle of size at least 4 and every hybrid node in a cycle of size at least 5" of a level-1 network are identifiable from gene tree quartets; Allman, Baños, Rhodes, [NANUQ, Algorithms Mol. Biol. 14 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13015-019-0159-2) ([arXiv 2019-05-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07050); abstract only): level-1 inference from quartet hypothesis tests and a quartet intertaxon distance. TINNiK's introduction sets its algorithm against these level-1 methods.
- Allman, Baños, Mitchell, Rhodes, [J. Math. Biol. 86 (2023), 10](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-022-01838-9) ([arXiv 2022-05-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03466)): "most features of the unrooted tree of blobs" are determined by the distribution of gene quartet topologies, with no assumption on the level, through a B-quartet inference rule. It names the difficulty the three papers meet in different ways: a B-quartet of the network can have a cut-edge split on its induced 4-taxon network, "the main obstacle". Section 2 gives what is proved from it.
- Xu, Ané, [J. Math. Biol. 86 (2023), 12](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-022-01847-8) ([arXiv 2021-10-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11814)): the "distance split tree" built from average pairwise distances refines the tree of blobs, and at level 1 equals the tree of blobs refined to separate polytomies from blobs (Theorem 10). Its Conjecture 9 is taken up by a preprint under Since the paper. ECToBlob's §10 suggests applying its framework to distances.
- Solís-Lemus, Yang, Ané, [Syst. Biol. 65 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syw030): species tree methods can be inconsistent under gene flow. Dinh, Baños, [Mol. Biol. Evol. 42 (2025), msaf049](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaf049) (published 2025-03-07; abstract only): under the NMSC, "including nonanomalous scenarios", ASTRAL can produce a tree "that does not correspond to any topology displayed by the true underlying network". ECToBlob cites both as the reason a statement about quartet optima on networks is needed (its [14, 37]); TOB-QMC cites Dinh and Baños, with Molloy, Durvasula, Sankararaman 2021 (its [12], [27]).
- Ané, Fogg, Allman, Baños, Rhodes, [J. Math. Biol. 88 (2024), 29](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-024-02050-7) ([bioRxiv 2023-08-21](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.08.18.553582); abstract only): under common inheritance "4-taxon networks are never anomalous"; under independent and correlated inheritance simulations find "truly anomalous networks are rare" and many networks close to the anomaly zone. Source of the no-anomaly hypotheses of TOB-QMC and ECToBlob, and of the exchangeability of Section 2.
- Rhodes, Baños, Xu, Ané, [Adv. Appl. Math. 163 (2025), 102804](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2024.102804) ([arXiv 2024-02-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11693)): circular orders of outer-labeled planar blobs are identified from 4-taxon subnetworks. Its Corollary 6.6 gives the tree of blobs under the displayed tree model, common inheritance, and independent inheritance with no anomalous quartets. Its Lemma A.1 classifies 4-taxon networks, and ECToBlob says of its Corollary 4.2 that it extends part of that lemma to more taxa.
- Huber, van Iersel, Jones, Moulton, Veenema-Nipius, [Bull. Math. Biol. 87 (2025), 136](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-025-01510-5) ([arXiv 2024-08-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12997)). Theorem 5.1, for binary semi-directed networks, which "does not put any restriction on the level": a nontrivial `A|B` is a cut-edge split exactly when `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` is a cut-edge split of the quarnet for all `a₁, a₂ ∈ A`, `b₁, b₂ ∈ B`. Corollary 5.6: the quarnets determine the blob tree. TOB-QMC's Lemma 2 is the forward direction, as TOB-QMC says.
- Frohn, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, Jones, Kelk, [J. Comput. Syst. Sci. 152 (2025), 103655](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcss.2025.103655) ([arXiv 2024-09-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06034)). Lemma 1 sharpens Huber et al.'s theorem by fixing `a₁` and `b₁`. Theorem 5 reconstructs the tree of blobs of a binary network of any level in `O(n³)` time from `O(n³)` quarnet-splits, and Proposition 6 gives a lower bound of `Ω(n log n + ℓn)` quarnet-splits at level `ℓ`. For binary networks ECToBlob's Corollary 4.2 is this Lemma 1, as ECToBlob says: it "follows from Lemma 1 of [17], which depends on results in [22]. Our proof is considerably briefer and we relax the binary condition". ECToBlob's Theorem 4.1 goes further in two ways: the network need not be binary, and the partitions `A₁, A₂` and `B₁, B₂` are arbitrary.

Huber et al. and Frohn et al. work from exact quarnet information, with no statistical model. Both appeared after the TINNiK preprint and do not build on its algorithm.

What the papers add. TINNiK ([bioRxiv 2024-04-24](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.04.20.590418)) turns the sketch of Allman et al. 2023 into an algorithm with a consistency theorem (Theorem 4) and writes "We know of no other proposed algorithm for tree of blobs inference from biological data"; ECToBlob and TOB-QMC both describe it as the first. TOB-QMC and ECToBlob add a different outline: an optimum of the quartet criterion refines the tree of blobs, and edges are then contracted by quartet tests. We found no earlier statement of that refinement result, and no earlier proof of TINNiK's Theorem 4. Section 2 lists the cited results the proofs use, including Mitchell et al. 2019 and Rhodes 2020.

### Concurrent work

- TOB-QMC and ECToBlob. First public versions: TOB-QMC [bioRxiv v1, 2025-11-06](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.05.686850), under the title "Fast and accurate tree of blobs reconstruction under the network multispecies coalescent"; ECToBlob [bioRxiv v1, 2026-05-31](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.28.728501). ECToBlob's statement: "When this work was well underway, we learned a different group was independently pursuing the same outline for tree of blobs inference", with its research dated to a residence at ICERM in Fall 2024, and thanks to Dai, Han and Molloy "for sharing their pre-publication work". TOB-QMC v5 does not mention ECToBlob; it thanks Ané, Allman, Baños and Rhodes for feedback.
  - Same result: an optimum of the quartet criterion refines the tree of blobs. TOB-QMC's Theorem 1 assumes the binary NMSC model and T-quartet-nonanomalous networks. ECToBlob's Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.6 assume only two conditions on the gene tree distribution, which its Remark 3.3 says hold for the NMSC with no anomalous quartets, for common inheritance and for the displayed tree model.
  - Same question by another method: the contraction step. TOB-QMC applies TINNiK's tests (ECToBlob compares with the T3 option) to `O(n)` four-taxon subsets around an edge and keeps the smallest p-value, with guarantees at level 1 (its Corollary 1, Theorem 2). ECToBlob applies the T1 test to the bipartition, quadripartition or multipartition sets of an edge, combines p-values under dependence, and contracts one step at a time; its Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 8.2 hold at any level. ECToBlob's §10 gives the authors' comparison of the two.
- Holtgrefe, Huber, van Iersel, Jones, Martin, Moulton, Squirrel, [Mol. Biol. Evol. 42 (2025), msaf067](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaf067) ([bioRxiv 2024-11-03](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.11.01.621567); abstract only): level-1 networks from quarnets or alignments, "combinatorially consistent" on full triangle-free quarnet sets. TOB-QMC says it was released during its study and "creates candidate TOBs, through edge contractions, which is similar to TOB-QMC", from sequence data and with no statistical guarantee under the NMSC.
- Englander, Frohn, Gross, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, Jones, Sullivant, [bioRxiv 2025-04-24](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.18.649493) (v4, 2026-07-04; preprint, no journal link in the bioRxiv API; abstract only): claims that under the Jukes-Cantor and Kimura 2-parameter models any two binary semi-directed networks displaying different quartets are distinguishable, so that the tree of blobs is identifiable at any level. Same question on sequence data. ECToBlob cites it.

- Dai, Molloy, *Is level-1 blob reconstruction under the network multispecies coalescent easy?*, [WABI 2026, LIPIcs 390, 1:1–1:24](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.WABI.2026.1) (published 2026-08-27, revised 2026-09-28; [bioRxiv v1 2026-06-10, v2 2026-09-21](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.06.730607)). Treelikeness beyond level 1. TOB-QMC's oracle asks only whether the network induced on four taxa has a 4-blob. No work we read proves or refutes that this suffices at higher level, on all four-taxon subsets around an edge.

- TOB-QMC v5 proves it at level 1 (Corollary 1, Theorem 2), and from level-2 simulations suggests "a practical heuristic for higher-level networks". Its supplement of February 26, 2026 claims it for disjoint covers at any level (its Lemma 7, Theorem 1); that claim is false at level 2 (E25).
- ECToBlob's §10: "we know of no proof of a stronger result that would justify using the T3 test. Thus the formal justification for TOB-QMC is currently limited to level-1 networks [13, Theorem 2], although simulations suggest it may perform well beyond that case."
- Huber et al.'s Theorem 5.1, Frohn et al.'s Lemma 1 and ECToBlob's Theorem 4.1 hold at any level, but they ask whether a named split `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` is a cut-edge split of the quarnet, which is more than treelikeness: Frohn et al.'s count of a quarnet-split includes identifying "the specific split".
- Allman et al. 2023 and TINNiK work at any level and use resolved topologies with the inference rule, since "the induced network on a B-quartet may or may not have a 4-blob, and can even be a tree". They do not ask about four-taxon sets around an edge of a refinement.
- Dai and Molloy's WABI paper, "Beyond level-1" and Holtgrefe et al. do not address the question. The works read only by abstract, among them BROOQS, DIMPLE and Justison et al., do not mention it there; their texts were not read.

## 11. What's next

Plans for follow-up papers are kept in `NEXT.md`, which is not published.

### Open directions

- **Treelikeness beyond level 1.** ECToBlob's §10 says of the T3 test: "we know of no proof of a stronger result that would justify using the T3 test", and that the formal justification for TOB-QMC stops at level 1; Section 10 finds no published work that settles this. The question: for a network of any level and a refinement `B` of its tree of blobs, does every edge of `B` not in the tree of blobs have a non-treelike 4-taxon set among those around it?
- **Assumption 1 under correlated inheritance.** ECToBlob's §5 says "We strongly suspect Assumption 1 also holds for the NMSC model with common or correlated inheritance". For binary networks at generic parameters, "Beyond level-1" (Proposition 5.1, Section 10) extends the arguments of Allman et al. 2023 to common inheritance, `ρ = 1`, for tree of blobs identifiability, and leaves other `ρ` aside. So what is open is `0 < ρ < 1`, and `ρ = 1` on non-binary networks with ECToBlob's measure. [`Corollary1`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Basic.lean#L63) here covers only `ρ = 0`. [`Phylogenetics.geneTreeDist`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Defs.lean#L94) includes `ρ`, and the equalities of the forward clauses are proved for all `ρ` ([`Phylogenetics.NMSC.cut_alternatives`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Exchangeability.lean#L550), [`Phylogenetics.NMSC.trivial_uniform`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Exchangeability.lean#L771)); the strict inequality of (5.2) is the no-anomaly hypothesis. What is needed is the generic clause: a nonvanishing witness for each 4-taxon network, for each `ρ`.
- **Rates.** All consistency statements here are asymptotic, with no rate. The proof of Theorem 8.2 runs on bounds per test, and could run on one event per quartet (below), so a non-asymptotic statement would follow from a concentration bound on each test, with constants depending on the distance of the CF from the null. What stands in the way is the non-regularity at the centroid and the boundary (E11, E13).
- **Theorem 8.2 for Cauchy and CBC on the boundary.** E11 shows these two variants need positive alternatives on null quartets. The T1 test's own null excludes the boundary in Mitchell et al., while (5.2) does not. A test that behaves at `(1,0,0)` would remove the hypothesis, as would a finite value of the Cauchy transform at `p = 1` (E11). An assumption that excludes the boundary would too, but it would drop the Displayed Tree model from these two variants, since a quartet with a cut-edge split has CF `(1,0,0)` there.
- **Coarsening, when Remark 8.4 fails.** E35 settles the remark, and [`ECToBlob.Coarsening.limitTree`](../src/ECToBlob/Remark84.lean#L822) is what the algorithm tends to. What should the algorithm do on a non-binary network when `T₀` isn't a resolution? A test that sees the false edge in E35 would have to use quartets beyond those around it.
- **The no-anomaly assumption.** ECToBlob's §10 would like to drop it. For its T1 test this is done, as it says, by the test of the line alone ([`ECToBlob.Remarks.consistency_line_nmsc`](../src/ECToBlob/Remarks.lean#L566)); it is easier than T1, since the centroid is interior to the line, so E13 disappears. The step that remains is the resolution: without the assumption, an optimum of the quartet criterion need not refine the tree of blobs, and ECToBlob asks for an algorithm that uses the CF entry that differs from the others: "Developing an algorithm to give a resolution of the tree of blobs based on that fact would be valuable". We found no work that does this.

### Simpler proofs

Each of these is the argument of the formal proof, so it is checked in Lean. We found none of them published, in the search of Section 10.

- **ECToBlob Theorem 4.1, in domination language.** An arc `e` induces the split `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` on a quartet exactly when `e` dominates `a₁, a₂` (every root path to them uses `e`) and `b₁, b₂` are not below it, or the reverse ([`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.hasCutSplitOn_iff_separates`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Network/Cuts.lean#L253)). So a cut edge of a quartet is a statement about each taxon separately. The paper's minimal choice of quadruple stays; the funnel step becomes the comparability that E4 finds unstated: replacing one taxon by another of its block gives comparable sets of cut arcs, by four cases on how two arcs are oriented, ordered when they dominate a common taxon. This saves the funnels, the graph `G̃₄`, Figure 2 and the "two 3-blobs" remark: about 300 lines against about 1,070. An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the paper.
- **Allman et al. Lemma 3, likewise.** Since `e₀` is a cut on `Y \ {α}` and not on `Y`, the taxon `α` is below `e₀` and not dominated by it, and an arc resolving `{a,b,c,α}` on `Y \ {d}` compares with `e₀` by `External.AllmanEtAl2023.order_of_isBelow_of_dominates`, in four cases. Components and the cycle through `A, K_ab, e₀, K_cd` do not appear. It takes about 126 lines against about 1,600 for the source's route. An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the source. Smaller changes also remain possible within the source's route: `C` as a minimal subset of all of `P₁ ∪ P₂ ∪ P₃ ∪ P₄` closing up `e₀` must leave `K_ab` at `x`, so `P₁` itself is the path from `α` to `C`; and `y` reaches `α ≠ d`, so `P₃` can run straight to `y`, with no `z`.
- **TINNiK's Theorem 4: the centroid.** The paper proves `CF ≠ (1/3,1/3,1/3)` for generic parameters by a separate analyticity argument. It follows from CF-detectability: strictly cut and non-cut CFs each have two different entries. This removes that paragraph of the proof, and also the need for a separate generic set. The formal proof of Theorem 4 takes the paper's step; [`TINNiK.tests_correct`](../src/TINNiK/Basic.lean#L538) takes the shortcut, [`TINNiK.noCentroidCF_of_cfDetectable`](../src/TINNiK/Basic.lean#L384).
- **Allman et al. Theorem 1, part (b).** Instead of one parameter with three distinct CFs, take each pair of quartet trees separately. The reduction shows that the display trees of a non-treelike 4-set show at least two different quartets, so at a display tree with long arcs one of the pair has CF near 1. Non-cut then holds generically with no mixture in `γ`. This is a sketch; the formal proof follows the source's mixture.
- **Allman et al. Theorem 1, the choice of `γ`.** One explicit point suffices: all lengths 3 and `γ = 1/3`. Each endpoint CF is within `exp(-3) < 1/4` of a vertex, so the mixture has entries near `1/3`, `2/3`, `0`, with no genericity at all. An earlier version of the formal proof did this; it now follows the source.
- **Allman et al. Theorem 1, the contradiction.** With no blobs: `e₁` and `e₂` dominate a common taxon, so they are ordered, and in four cases by direction the arc nearer the blob has `a` on the `b` side in both deletions. An earlier version of the formal proof did this.
- **Allman et al. Corollary 1.** On `N` directly: statements (a) and (b) hold for a 4-set of any network, since domination off `D` sees the four taxa alone and CFs are analytic in `N`'s coordinates. This needs neither the induced network, nor the map of parameters, nor the identity of CFs. `External.AllmanEtAl2023.isGenericNull_not_isStrictlyCut` and `External.AllmanEtAl2023.isGenericNull_not_isNonCut` are stated so, and an earlier version derived Corollary 1 from them.
- **Allman et al. Theorem 2.** Take a side `A` of the tree of blobs with two taxa of the set: the two B-quartets constrain `A` on `a, b, c, d, e`, and 32 cases leave only `A` separating `a, e` from `b, c, d`, which is the contradiction. No node of the tree of blobs is needed. An earlier version of the formal proof did this.
- **ECToBlob Theorem 8.2, one event.** One event per quartet, Lemma 8.1 applied once to all null quartets, a joint tightness bound at the centroid and growth under the alternative give one `δ` and `c₀` that make every combined test on every possible tree come out right together. So E16 disappears, with no count `K` and no bound `C(s,4)`. And [`ECToBlob.falseEdge_witness`](../src/ECToBlob/FalseEdge.lean#L1044) alone gives a quartet in the multipartition of `T₀`, which serves all types, so Corollary 4.2 isn't needed. An earlier version of the formal proof did this; it now follows the paper.
- **ECToBlob Theorem 3.1.** Only the splice is needed of the attachments at lines 495–497: [`Phylogenetics.displays_splice_iff`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/Splice.lean#L159) gives its inner sums directly.
- **Rhodes Theorem 2.** `{x,y,u,v} ∈ Q` holds exactly when no side contains `u, v` and avoids `x, y`, so count such pairs directly and split them by node, with no case split on the shape of the quartet.
- **TOB-QMC's ASTRAL corollary.** Every binary gene tree occurs almost surely, so all binary trees are in the search space; there's no need for the probability of a bipartition.
- **TOB-QMC's supplement Lemma 8.** A lowest reticulation of the blob has only bridges below it, in any phylogenetic network.
- **The T3 test at the singularity.** Non-collapse needs no limit law: the T3 statistic is at most the T1 statistic, which is tight on the null, and the p-value is at least `½ χ²₁(10 t)`, since `sin² α₀ ≥ 1/10` on `[0,1]`.
- **NANUQ's Theorem 36.** Explicit levels prove it directly ([`ECToBlob.remark83_nanuq_fixed_beta`](../src/ECToBlob/Remark83.lean#L156)), with no levels built from the unknown `d`s. The formal [`External.AllmanBanosRhodes2019.theorem36`](../src/External/AllmanBanosRhodes2019/Proposition9.lean#L1607) keeps the source's route.
- **Neighbor joining.** The side with at most `n/2` clusters is shorter than Studier and Keppler's two-node contradiction, and it covers polytomies.
- **Remark 3.3's displayed tree model.** It is the limit of `ρ = 1` as all lengths grow ([`ECToBlob.abs_nmscCF_sub_displayedCF_le`](../src/ECToBlob/Remark33.lean#L461)), which also justifies the star convention, since a polytomy gives exactly `1/3` at all lengths.
- **TOB-QMC Lemma 3, the second observation.** Lines 489–492 restrict to one taxon per block and say a refinement's gain "would be amplified after reattaching". No restriction is needed: a maximizer resolves every 4-set in distinct blocks, since adding one split that resolves one more gains a positive CF on `X` directly, and that the result is binary then follows from the standard fact that a tree is binary exactly when it resolves every 4-set ([`Phylogenetics.isBinaryTree_of_forall_displays`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/Detach.lean#L177), [`Phylogenetics.IsBinaryTree.exists_displays`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/9948de9b3cba330596424efd2dca9617be2efb0c/Phylogenetics/Tree/Splits.lean#L589)). An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the paper. Similarly, the contradiction at lines 472–486 adds nothing to the direct transformation it contains.
- **TOB-QMC Lemma 4.** Read on the rooted induced subgraph, with no wedge paths: for ⇐, a blob arc does not dominate the taxon below the reticulation, and an arc away from the blob has the whole blob below it or two taxa in distinct blocks joined outside it; for ⇒, descend from the top of the blob to an arc with exactly two taxa below it. An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the paper, through wedge paths and the bridge [`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.hasWedgeCutSplit_iff_hasCutSplitOn`](../src/TOBQMC/Wedge.lean#L900).
- **TINNiK Proposition 6.** Computing the strip probabilities in rotated Cartesian coordinates, with Fubini in place of differentiating under the integral, and taking `G₄, G₅` as reflections of `G₃, G₂` at `-μ`, gives the same `d/dx Gᵢ` and needs no domination argument. An earlier version of the formal proof did this (in the repository's history); the formal proof now follows the paper.
- **Allman et al. Theorem 3.** The first pass to one taxon per component isn't needed: removing a taxon or applying Lemma 3 covers every case. Checked in a scratch Lean file, outside the library.
- **Wilks-type limits for a trinomial.** The papers cite these limits; for a trinomial there is an elementary proof. One estimate, `|(1+v) log(1+v) - v - v²/2| ≤ 4|v|³` for `|v| ≤ 1/2` ([`Real.abs_entropyExcess_sub_le`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/EntropyExcess.lean#L50)), gives each likelihood ratio statistic as a sum of such terms, so each is uniformly close on bounded sets to a continuous function of the whitened frequencies, and an extended continuous mapping theorem, proved from the portmanteau theorem on open sets (`Phylogenetics.Trinomial.tendstoInDistribution_of_closeOnBalls`), finishes. This does not use van der Vaart's Theorem 16.7. At the centroid, Pearson's statistic is exactly `Z₁² + Z₂²` ([`Phylogenetics.Trinomial.pearsonS_centroid`](../src/Basic/Testing/Trinomial/Whiten.lean#L425)); the T1 test at the centroid is handled separately (E13).
