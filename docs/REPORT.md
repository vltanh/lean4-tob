# Inferring the tree of blobs: an overview of the research direction

The four papers formalized in this repository serve one problem: inferring the tree of blobs of a phylogenetic network. This report is an overview of that direction: the problem and its history (Section 1), the papers, their results and methods, and how they relate (Section 2), the literature of the field (Section 3), the open directions that span the papers (Section 4), where the audit stands (Section 5), and the objects that the papers share and how the formalization reads them (Section 6). The audit of each paper is in its own report, in `papers/<Paper>/REPORT.md`, with the paper's errors and gaps, its missing and redundant hypotheses, the readings and the departures from its proofs, what each result depends on, what isn't formalized, and its own place in the literature.

## 1. The problem and its history

A phylogenetic network on a set of taxa `X` is a finite directed acyclic graph with a root that reaches every vertex, whose sinks are the taxa. Its reticulations, the vertices with more than one parent, stand for hybridization, gene flow or recombination. A blob is a maximal connected subgraph without cut edges. Contracting each blob to a vertex gives a tree, and suppressing its vertices of degree two gives the tree of blobs, whose splits are the splits of `X` by the cut edges of the network. In the library [lean4-phylogenetics](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics), [`Phylogenetics.treeOfBlobs`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/Quartets.lean#L40) is this family of splits and [`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.blobTree`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/TreeOfBlobs.lean#L127) the contracted graph, and the two agree. The level of a blob is its number of reticulation edges minus its number of reticulation vertices, and a network is level-1 when all its blobs have level at most one.

The tree of blobs shows the tree-like parts of the history and where reticulation happened, and leaves out the inside of each blob. The data are gene trees, one per locus. Under the network multispecies coalescent (NMSC), each gene tree forms by coalescence inside the network, with branch lengths in coalescent units and inheritance probabilities at the reticulations. The lineages at a reticulation take their parents independently at `ρ = 0` (independent inheritance), all take one parent at `ρ = 1` (common inheritance), and are correlated in between. In the displayed tree model, with no coalescent process, a gene tree comes from choosing one parent at every vertex, with the inheritance probabilities. The concordance factor of four taxa is the distribution of the three unrooted gene tree topologies on them. The question has two parts. Identifiability: do the concordance factors determine the tree of blobs? Inference: is there an algorithm that, from a sample of gene trees, returns the tree of blobs with probability tending to one as the sample grows?

The history in brief, with the sources in Section 3:

- **The combinatorial origin, 1985 to 2007.** Lower bounds on the number of recombinations (Hudson, Kaplan 1985; Myers, Griffiths 2003), perfect phylogenetic networks with recombination (Wang, Zhang, Zhang 2001), galled trees (Gusfield, Eddhu, Langley 2004), and the decomposition theorem for networks with recombination and incompatible characters (Gusfield, Bansal, RECOMB 2005; Gusfield, Bansal, Bafna, Song 2007), whose blobs correspond to the components of the incompatibility graph.
- **Level-1 networks from quartets, 2016 to 2019.** Pseudolikelihood on quartets (SNaQ, Solís-Lemus, Ané 2016), identifiability of level-1 features from gene tree quartets (Baños 2019), and inference from quartet hypothesis tests and a quartet intertaxon distance (NANUQ, Allman, Baños, Rhodes 2019). Species tree methods can be inconsistent under gene flow (Solís-Lemus, Yang, Ané 2016).
- **The tree of blobs at any level, 2022 to 2025.** Allman, Baños, Mitchell, Rhodes (arXiv 2022, published 2023) proved that quartet concordance factors determine the tree of blobs with no assumption on the level. TINNiK (bioRxiv and journal, 2024) made it an algorithm with a consistency theorem. Huber et al. 2025 and Frohn et al. 2025 work from exact quarnet information, with no statistical model.
- **Two concurrent methods, 2025 to 2026.** TOB-QMC (bioRxiv 2025-11-06) and ECToBlob (bioRxiv 2026-05-31) show that an optimum of a quartet criterion, which quartet-based species tree methods compute, refines the tree of blobs, and remove its false edges by tests. Other work starts from a tree of blobs to infer the blobs themselves.

## 2. The papers, their results and methods, and how they relate

### 2.1 The decomposition theory: Gusfield et al. 2007

Gusfield, Bansal, Bafna and Song work with networks with recombination that derive a set `M` of binary sequences at their leaves, under a model of crossover. A blob is the union of a class of recombination cycles, obtained from one cycle by repeatedly adding a cycle that shares an edge with one already there. The incompatibility graph `G(M)` has the sites as vertices and joins incompatible sites. Their Decomposition Theorem (Theorem 1): a nonempty `M` with no constant column has a network in which every blob contains all and only the sites of one non-trivial connected component of `G(M)`, and every compatible site is on a tree edge. The proof goes by super-characters, built from the components, and a tree `T̄` on which each super-character labels an edge, obtained by contracting the tree that the Perfect Phylogeny Theorem (Gusfield 1991) gives. The paper then asks whether a network with the fewest recombination nodes can always be taken fully decomposed. It gives sufficient conditions (Theorems 5 to 10), and refutes the four Full-Decomposition Optimality Conjectures (Section 5.3, Theorem 11).

This is where blobs come from. Allman et al. 2023 and TOB-QMC cite it for the tree of blobs, and the later papers take the notion from there or from Allman et al. Its networks carry sites and sequences, and those of the four papers here carry branch lengths and inheritance probabilities. No proof here uses it. It is formalized separately, with the results it cites, in [lean4-gusfield2007](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-gusfield2007), whose [report](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-gusfield2007/blob/main/REPORT.md) is its audit.

### 2.2 Identifiability: Allman et al. 2023

Allman, Baños, Mitchell and Rhodes ask what the distribution of gene quartet topologies determines under the NMSC, with no assumption on the level. Their objects are binary networks ([`AllmanEtAl2023.IsBinaryNetwork`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Definitions.lean#L61), Definition 1), the network induced on a set of taxa (Definition 6, [`AllmanEtAl2023.induced`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/InducedNetwork.lean#L296)), the reduced unrooted tree of blobs ([`AllmanEtAl2023.Trd`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/BlobDefinitions.lean#L422)), the concordance factor `CF_abcd` ([`AllmanEtAl2023.cfTriple`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Concordance.lean#L66)) and generic parameters ([`AllmanEtAl2023.ForGenericParams`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Concordance.lean#L282), parameters outside a null set).

- **On four taxa.** Lemma 1: the semidirected network has one 4-blob, a B-quartet, or two 3-blobs, a T-quartet, and otherwise only 2-blobs. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, the CF-detectability of 4-blobs: for generic parameters, the tree of blobs of the induced 4-taxon network has the topology `ab|cd` exactly when the concordance factor is strictly `(ab|cd)`-cut, and is unresolved exactly when it is non-cut. Proposition 1 and its converse: the concordance factors of such networks with topology `ab|cd` are exactly the triples `(p, q, q)`.
- **From quartets to the whole network.** A B-quartet of the network can have a cut-edge split on its induced 4-taxon network, which the paper calls "the main obstacle". The B-quartet Inference Rule (Theorem 2) and its closure (Theorem 3) identify all B-quartets from the concordance factors. Theorem 4: for generic parameters, one function, chosen before the network, recovers the reduced unrooted tree of blobs from the gene quartet distribution.
- **Methods.** The proofs of Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 combine the analytic dependence of the concordance factors on the parameters, the null zero sets of analytic functions, bounds at boundary parameters, and a reduction of the network that deletes arcs ([`AllmanEtAl2023.exists_reduction_aux`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Reduction.lean#L673)). Section 5 outlines an algorithm ([`TreeOfBlobs.allman_outline_consistent`](../src/MainResults/AllmanEtAl2023.lean#L172)).

The later papers use this paper as a source. TINNiK numbers its CF-detectability Theorem 1 and its inference rule Theorem 2. TOB-QMC's Lemma 1 uses its reduction, and TOB-QMC's consistency claim its CF-detectability. ECToBlob's Proposition 5.1 adapts its Theorem 1 by "checking the argument", which is proved here as [`ECToBlob.adaptedAllmanTheorem1`](../src/ECToBlob/Section5.lean#L151), for ECToBlob's measure on networks whose blob vertices have degree at most 3. The forms that the later papers use are stated on subgraphs of a fixed network, among them [`AllmanEtAl2023.cfDetectable_generic`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/CFDetectable.lean#L24), [`AllmanEtAl2023.rule_core`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Basic.lean#L614), [`AllmanEtAl2023.closure_sound`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Basic.lean#L732), [`AllmanEtAl2023.closure_complete`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Basic.lean#L724) and [`AllmanEtAl2023.lemma3_resolves`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Basic.lean#L240).

### 2.3 An algorithm: TINNiK

TINNiK turns the sketch of Allman et al. 2023 into an algorithm with a consistency theorem, and writes "We know of no other proposed algorithm for tree of blobs inference from biological data"; ECToBlob and TOB-QMC both describe it as the first. NANUQ (Allman, Baños, Rhodes 2019) infers level-1 networks from quartet hypothesis tests and a quartet intertaxon distance, and ECToBlob describes NANUQ and TINNiK as algorithms "which use quartet hypothesis tests in a similar way". TINNiK needs no bound on the level.

- **The algorithm** ([`tinnik`](../src/Basic/Defs.lean#L161)). From the counts of quartet topologies among the gene trees, a set of four taxa is judged a B-quartet when the star test fails to reject at level `β`, or the cut test rejects at level `α`; the T3 test is an option. The judged sets are closed under the B-quartet Inference Rule. A quartet distance (Rhodes 2020) is given to an exact tree builder, such as neighbor joining, and edges shorter than a cutoff `δ` are contracted.
- **The tests.** They build on Mitchell, Allman, Rhodes 2019, on hypothesis testing near singularities and boundaries. TINNiK's Proposition 5 is the limit law of the likelihood ratio at a cut model, from their Theorem 3.1, and Proposition 6 its density.
- **Theorem 4.** For generic parameters on a binary network, there is a sequence `α_m → 0` such that, for any `β > 0`, any exact tree builder and any selection of maximum likelihood topologies, the algorithm on `m` independent gene trees returns the tree of blobs with probability tending to one. By Theorem 2 of Rhodes 2020, the quartet distance is a tree metric with the topology of the tree, so an exact tree builder recovers it.

ECToBlob's Remark 8.3 adapts the argument of its Theorem 8.2 to strengthen the consistency results of NANUQ and TINNiK, which "makes explicit some valid choices of sequences of test levels for those algorithms" ([`ECToBlob.remark83_tinnik`](../src/ECToBlob/Remark83.lean#L235), [`ECToBlob.remark83_nanuq`](../src/ECToBlob/Remark83.lean#L102)).

### 2.4 Two concurrent methods: TOB-QMC and ECToBlob

TOB-QMC and ECToBlob follow the same outline, independently: an optimum of a quartet criterion refines the tree of blobs, and edges are then contracted by tests on quartets around them. ECToBlob says: "When this work was well underway, we learned a different group was independently pursuing the same outline for tree of blobs inference". Section 3.5 has the dates.

- **The refinement.** TOB-QMC's Theorem 1: under the binary NMSC, on networks whose T-quartets are not anomalous, every optimal solution of Weighted Quartet Consensus refines the tree of blobs, almost surely for all large numbers of gene trees. So quartet-based species tree methods, TREE-QMC and ASTRAL among them, give a refinement. ECToBlob's Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.6: every tree maximizing the Maximum Quartet Support objective resolves the tree of blobs, under two conditions on the gene tree distribution, strict dominance across cut edges and exchangeability across them. Its Remark 3.3 says these hold for the NMSC with no anomalous quartets, for common inheritance and for the displayed tree model. The no-anomaly hypotheses come from Ané et al. 2024, and so does the exchangeability across a cut edge.
- **Which quartets decide an edge.** TOB-QMC's Lemma 2 is the forward direction of Theorem 5.1 of Huber et al. 2025. Its Theorem 2: on a binary level-1 network, an oracle for treelikeness on a disjoint cover of quartets around each edge of a refinement finds the edges to contract. ECToBlob's Theorem 4.1, at any level: if `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` is a cut-edge split of every induced 4-taxon network for `aᵢ ∈ Aᵢ`, `bᵢ ∈ Bᵢ`, then `A₁ ∪ A₂ | B₁ ∪ B₂` is a cut-edge split of the network. For binary networks its Corollary 4.2 is Lemma 1 of Frohn et al. 2025, as ECToBlob says.
- **The contraction step.** TOB-QMC applies TINNiK's tests to `O(n)` four-taxon subsets around an edge, keeps the smallest p-value, and has its guarantees at level 1: Corollary 1, Theorem 2, and the consistency claim of §4.3, with the levels of the proof of TINNiK's Theorem 4. ECToBlob applies the T1 test to the bipartition, quadripartition or multipartition sets of an edge, combines p-values under dependence, and contracts one step at a time. Its Theorem 8.2: there are test levels, independent of the network, such that every variant of the algorithm, from any resolution of the tree of blobs, returns the tree of blobs with probability tending to one. ECToBlob's §10 gives the authors' comparison of the two methods.

Whether treelikeness of the quartets around an edge decides it beyond level 1 is open (Sections 3.7 and 4.1).

Weller, Zeh, [arXiv 2508.21749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21749) (preprint), citing a manuscript in preparation by van Iersel, Jones, Landry, Linz, Weller, Zeh and Zhang, say it fails for four or more trees. These preprints disagree, we haven't read the manuscript, and we report them as claimed.

**Since: generalizations.** Dress, Huber, Koolen, Moulton, [SIAM J. Discrete Math. 25 (2011), 1902–1919](https://doi.org/10.1137/090764360) ([arXiv 1101.4927](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.4927)): the blocks of the Buneman graph of a split system correspond one to one to the connected components of its incompatibility graph (Theorem 5.1), and a graph built on them is a tree (Theorem 6.1), generalizing that the Buneman graph is a tree exactly when the splits are compatible. They say the blocks "are also closely related to the 'blobs' defined in terms of directed graphs described by D. Gusfield et al.". This is the route by Buneman graphs that line 350 mentions. Read side by side, their reduced tree `T_Σ` looks like `T̄`; neither paper says so, and we didn't check it further. Lam, Gusfield, Sridhar, [SIAM J. Discrete Math. 25 (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1137/090776305), generalize the Splits Equivalence Theorem and the four-gamete condition to three-state characters; title and abstract only. Allman et al. 2023 cite this paper for the tree of blobs, and the later papers take it from there or from this paper. We found no later study that repeats the empirical claim of Table 1.

### 3.3 Networks from quartets under the coalescent

### 3.4 Identifiability of the tree of blobs, and four-taxon information at any level

- Allman, Baños, Mitchell, Rhodes, [J. Math. Biol. 86 (2023), 10](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-022-01838-9) ([arXiv 2022-05-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03466)): "most features of the unrooted tree of blobs" are determined by the distribution of gene quartet topologies, with no assumption on the level, through a B-quartet inference rule. It names the difficulty the three papers meet in different ways: a B-quartet of the network can have a cut-edge split on its induced 4-taxon network, "the main obstacle". It is formalized here: [its report](../papers/AllmanEtAl2023/REPORT.md). Versions: arXiv [2205.03466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03466) has v1 only (2022-05-06), and differs from the PMC manuscript as Section 6 of its report says. Crossref and Europe PMC list no correction. We couldn't reach Springer's text, so whether the journal has the misprints of E57 and E66 is unknown. Nothing we read cites those, the connectivity that Lemma 2 needs, or the unused sentence of E57. We found no work that restates Lemma 2 or the proof of Theorem 3, so nothing later corrects them. The citing works we screened otherwise cite Theorem 4 or the definitions.
- Xu, Ané, [J. Math. Biol. 86 (2023), 12](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-022-01847-8) ([arXiv 2021-10-22](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11814)): the "distance split tree" built from average pairwise distances refines the tree of blobs, and at level 1 equals the tree of blobs refined to separate polytomies from blobs (Theorem 10). Its Conjecture 9 is taken up by a preprint in Section 3.6. ECToBlob's §10 suggests applying its framework to distances.
- Ané, Fogg, Allman, Baños, Rhodes, [J. Math. Biol. 88 (2024), 29](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-024-02050-7) ([bioRxiv 2023-08-21](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.08.18.553582)): under common inheritance "4-taxon networks are never anomalous"; under independent and correlated inheritance simulations find "truly anomalous networks are rare" and many networks close to the anomaly zone. Source of the no-anomaly hypotheses of TOB-QMC and ECToBlob, and of the exchangeability across a cut edge that both use, proved in [`src/External/AneEtAl2024/`](../src/External/AneEtAl2024).
- Rhodes, Baños, Xu, Ané, [Adv. Appl. Math. 163 (2025), 102804](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2024.102804) ([arXiv 2024-02-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11693)): circular orders of outer-labeled planar blobs are identified from 4-taxon subnetworks. Its Corollary 6.6 gives the tree of blobs under the displayed tree model, common inheritance, and independent inheritance with no anomalous quartets. Its Lemma A.1 classifies 4-taxon networks, and ECToBlob says of its Corollary 4.2 that it extends part of that lemma to more taxa.
- Huber, van Iersel, Jones, Moulton, Veenema-Nipius, [Bull. Math. Biol. 87 (2025), 136](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-025-01510-5) ([arXiv 2024-08-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12997)). Theorem 5.1, for binary semi-directed networks, which "does not put any restriction on the level": a nontrivial `A|B` is a cut-edge split exactly when `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` is a cut-edge split of the quarnet for all `a₁, a₂ ∈ A`, `b₁, b₂ ∈ B`. Corollary 5.6: the quarnets determine the blob tree. TOB-QMC's Lemma 2 is the forward direction, as TOB-QMC says.
- Frohn, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, Jones, Kelk, [J. Comput. Syst. Sci. 152 (2025), 103655](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcss.2025.103655) ([arXiv 2024-09-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06034)). Lemma 1 sharpens Huber et al.'s theorem by fixing `a₁` and `b₁`. Theorem 5 reconstructs the tree of blobs of a binary network of any level in `O(n³)` time from `O(n³)` quarnet-splits, and Proposition 6 gives a lower bound of `Ω(n log n + ℓn)` quarnet-splits at level `ℓ`. For binary networks ECToBlob's Corollary 4.2 is this Lemma 1, as ECToBlob says: it "follows from Lemma 1 of [17], which depends on results in [22]. Our proof is considerably briefer and we relax the binary condition". ECToBlob's Theorem 4.1 goes further in two ways: the network need not be binary, and the partitions `A₁, A₂` and `B₁, B₂` are arbitrary.

Huber et al. and Frohn et al. work from exact quarnet information, with no statistical model. Both appeared after the TINNiK preprint and do not build on its algorithm.

### 3.5 Algorithms: TINNiK, TOB-QMC and ECToBlob

**What the three papers add.** TINNiK ([bioRxiv 2024-04-24](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.04.20.590418)) turns the sketch of Allman et al. 2023 into an algorithm with a consistency theorem (Theorem 4) and writes "We know of no other proposed algorithm for tree of blobs inference from biological data"; ECToBlob and TOB-QMC both describe it as the first. TOB-QMC and ECToBlob add a different outline: an optimum of the quartet criterion refines the tree of blobs, and edges are then contracted by quartet tests. We found no earlier statement of that refinement result, and no earlier proof of TINNiK's Theorem 4. Section 2 of each paper's report lists the cited results its proofs use, among them Mitchell et al. 2019 and Rhodes 2020.

**Concurrent work.**

- TOB-QMC and ECToBlob. First public versions: TOB-QMC [bioRxiv v1, 2025-11-06](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.05.686850), under the title "Fast and accurate tree of blobs reconstruction under the network multispecies coalescent"; ECToBlob [bioRxiv v1, 2026-05-31](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.28.728501). ECToBlob's statement: "When this work was well underway, we learned a different group was independently pursuing the same outline for tree of blobs inference", with its research dated to a residence at ICERM in Fall 2024, and thanks to Dai, Han and Molloy "for sharing their pre-publication work". TOB-QMC v5 does not mention ECToBlob; it thanks Ané, Allman, Baños and Rhodes for feedback.
  - Same result: an optimum of the quartet criterion refines the tree of blobs. TOB-QMC's Theorem 1 assumes the binary NMSC model and T-quartet-nonanomalous networks. ECToBlob's Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.6 assume only two conditions on the gene tree distribution, which its Remark 3.3 says hold for the NMSC with no anomalous quartets, for common inheritance and for the displayed tree model.
  - Same question by another method: the contraction step. TOB-QMC applies TINNiK's tests (ECToBlob compares with the T3 option) to `O(n)` four-taxon subsets around an edge and keeps the smallest p-value, with guarantees at level 1 (its Corollary 1, Theorem 2). ECToBlob applies the T1 test to the bipartition, quadripartition or multipartition sets of an edge, combines p-values under dependence, and contracts one step at a time; its Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 8.2 hold at any level. ECToBlob's §10 gives the authors' comparison of the two.
- Holtgrefe, Huber, van Iersel, Jones, Martin, Moulton, Squirrel, [Mol. Biol. Evol. 42 (2025), msaf067](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msaf067) ([bioRxiv 2024-11-03](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.11.01.621567); abstract only): level-1 networks from quarnets or alignments, "combinatorially consistent" on full triangle-free quarnet sets. TOB-QMC says it was released during its study and "creates candidate TOBs, through edge contractions, which is similar to TOB-QMC", from sequence data and with no statistical guarantee under the NMSC.
- Englander, Frohn, Gross, Holtgrefe, van Iersel, Jones, Sullivant, [bioRxiv 2025-04-24](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.18.649493) (v4, 2026-07-04; preprint, no journal link in the bioRxiv API; abstract only): claims that under the Jukes-Cantor and Kimura 2-parameter models any two binary semi-directed networks displaying different quartets are distinguishable, so that the tree of blobs is identifiable at any level. Same question on sequence data. ECToBlob cites it.

**Versions of the three papers.**

- TOB-QMC: bioRxiv v2 (2025-12-09) changed the title; v3 (2026-02-26), v4 (2026-04-06), and v5 (2026-05-15), the version audited. The supplement's internal date, February 26, 2026, is v3's posting date. Published as [Genome Research, doi 10.1101/gr.282203.126](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.282203.126), accepted 2026-08-20, online 2026-08-28, [ahead of print on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42665444/). We have not read its text or supplement (subscription; the NSF Public Access Repository copy is embargoed to 2027-08-28), so we cannot say how its statements differ from v5.

### 3.7 Treelikeness beyond level 1

TOB-QMC's oracle asks only whether the network induced on four taxa has a 4-blob. No work we read proves or refutes that this suffices at higher level, on all four-taxon subsets around an edge.

- TOB-QMC v5 proves it at level 1 (Corollary 1, Theorem 2), and from level-2 simulations suggests "a practical heuristic for higher-level networks". Its supplement of February 26, 2026 claims it for disjoint covers at any level (its Lemma 7, Theorem 1); that claim is false at level 2 (E25).
- ECToBlob's §10: "we know of no proof of a stronger result that would justify using the T3 test. Thus the formal justification for TOB-QMC is currently limited to level-1 networks [13, Theorem 2], although simulations suggest it may perform well beyond that case."
- Huber et al.'s Theorem 5.1, Frohn et al.'s Lemma 1 and ECToBlob's Theorem 4.1 hold at any level, but they ask whether a named split `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` is a cut-edge split of the quarnet, which is more than treelikeness: Frohn et al.'s count of a quarnet-split includes identifying "the specific split".
- Allman et al. 2023 and TINNiK work at any level and use resolved topologies with the inference rule, since "the induced network on a B-quartet may or may not have a 4-blob, and can even be a tree". They do not ask about four-taxon sets around an edge of a refinement.
- Dai and Molloy's WABI paper, "Beyond level-1" and Holtgrefe et al. do not address the question. The works read only by abstract, among them BROOQS, DIMPLE and Justison et al., do not mention it there; their texts were not read.

## 4. Open directions

The directions in Section 4.1 span more than one paper. Those about one paper are in Section 11 of its report, and Section 4.2 lists them briefly.

### 4.1 Across the papers

- **Treelikeness beyond level 1.** ECToBlob's §10 says of the T3 test: "we know of no proof of a stronger result that would justify using the T3 test", and that the formal justification for TOB-QMC stops at level 1; Section 3.7 finds no published work that settles this. The question: for a network of any level and a refinement `B` of its tree of blobs, does every edge of `B` not in the tree of blobs have a non-treelike 4-taxon set among those around it? TOB-QMC's supplement claims it for disjoint covers at any level, and that claim is false at level 2 (E25).
- **Generic results under correlated inheritance.** [`AllmanEtAl2023.cfDetectable_generic`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/CFDetectable.lean#L24), which TINNiK's Theorem 4 uses, covers only `ρ = 0`, and so does [`AllmanEtAl2023.corollary1`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Corollary1.lean#L67). ECToBlob's §5 says "We strongly suspect Assumption 1 also holds for the NMSC model with common or correlated inheritance". For binary networks at generic parameters, "Beyond level-1" (Proposition 5.1, Section 3.6) extends the arguments of Allman et al. 2023 to common inheritance, `ρ = 1`, for tree of blobs identifiability, and leaves other `ρ` aside. So what is open is `0 < ρ < 1`, and `ρ = 1` on non-binary networks with ECToBlob's measure. [`Phylogenetics.geneTreeDist`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Defs.lean#L94) includes `ρ`, and the equalities of the forward clauses are proved for all `ρ` ([`Phylogenetics.NMSC.cut_alternatives`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Exchangeability.lean#L550), [`Phylogenetics.NMSC.trivial_uniform`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Exchangeability.lean#L771)); the strict inequality of (5.2) is the no-anomaly hypothesis. What is needed is the generic clause: a nonvanishing witness for each 4-taxon network, for each `ρ`. [`Phylogenetics.NMSC.one_sub_exp_le_nmscCF`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Witness.lean#L687) and the analytic parametrization hold for all `ρ`, and the mixture does too, so the same proof may reach the generic clause at other `ρ`.
- **The no-anomaly assumption.** The refinement results of TOB-QMC and ECToBlob, on the NMSC, assume it: T-quartet nonanomaly in TOB-QMC's Theorem 1, and no anomalous quartets in ECToBlob's Remark 3.3. ECToBlob's §10 would like to drop it. For its T1 test this is done, as it says, by the test of the line alone ([`ECToBlob.Remarks.consistency_line_nmsc`](../src/ECToBlob/Remarks.lean#L566)); it is easier than T1, since the centroid is interior to the line, so E13 disappears. The step that remains is the resolution: without the assumption, an optimum of the quartet criterion need not refine the tree of blobs, and ECToBlob asks for an algorithm that uses the CF entry that differs from the others: "Developing an algorithm to give a resolution of the tree of blobs based on that fact would be valuable". We found no work that does this.
- **Rates.** All consistency statements here, of TINNiK, TOB-QMC, ECToBlob and NANUQ, are asymptotic, with no rate. The proof of ECToBlob's Theorem 8.2 runs on bounds per test, and could run on one event per quartet (a simpler proof recorded in [ECToBlob's report](../papers/ECToBlob/REPORT.md)), so a non-asymptotic statement would follow from a concentration bound on each test, with constants depending on the distance of the CF from the null. What stands in the way is the non-regularity at the centroid and the boundary (E11, E13). Frohn et al.'s Ann. Comb. paper (Section 3.6) has a finite-sample bound for sequences at level 1, and we found none for the quartet tests of the three papers.
- **Non-binary networks.** [`TINNiK.theorem4`](../src/TINNiK/Basic.lean#L629) takes binary networks. [`AllmanEtAl2023.exists_reduction_of_blobDegree`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Reduction.lean#L951) should allow blob vertices of degree at most 3 and no trivial 4-blob on any 4-set; a trivial 4-blob has the centroid CF at every parameter, so some such condition is needed. ECToBlob's Theorem 4.1 holds without the binary condition, and its Remark 8.4 fails on a non-binary network (E35).
- **`T̄` and the tree of blobs.** A remark that is unverified: on paper, not in Lean, and read by no independent checker. For a binary network, `T̄` of all the splits of its displayed trees should be its tree of blobs, at any level. A split `A|B` of `T̄` is compatible with every displayed split, so every displayed tree shows `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` across it, so by [`AllmanEtAl2023.InPassing.isTreelike_iff_forall_displays`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/InPassing.lean#L513) and [`ECToBlob.theorem4_1`](../src/ECToBlob/Section4.lean#L55) (or Huber et al.'s Theorem 5.1) it is a cut-edge split. Conversely a cut-edge split is in every displayed tree and is a trivial component. A script tried it on 3,000 random binary networks with at most four reticulations; the script isn't kept in the repository. Under the displayed tree model `T̄` would then be an exact estimator. Under the coalescent, where every pair of gene-tree splits is eventually incompatible, is anything left of this?
- **A bridge between the two kinds of network.** The networks of Gusfield et al. 2007, [`Gusfield2007.Network`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-gusfield2007/blob/main/src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L129) in lean4-gusfield2007, carry sites and sequences, and the library's [`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/Defs.lean#L30) carries lengths and inheritance, and nothing connects them. The blobs and trees of blobs of the two could be compared, by forgetting the labels, in a new module that imports both. A Lean form of the remark on `T̄` above would need it.
- **Contributions to other libraries.** [`src/Basic/ForMathlib/`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib) holds what could move to Mathlib: the error function ([`src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Erf.lean`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Erf.lean)), the second-order expansion of `x log x` ([`src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/EntropyExcess.lean`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/EntropyExcess.lean)), the maximum of a multinomial likelihood ([`src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/GibbsInequality.lean`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/GibbsInequality.lean); Mathlib has Gibbs' inequality for `klDiv`), zero sets of analytic functions ([`src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/AnalyticZeroSet.lean`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/AnalyticZeroSet.lean)), stability of maximizers under perturbation ([`src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/FiniteOptimization.lean`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/FiniteOptimization.lean)), strip probabilities and the orthonormal change of coordinates for two independent unit normals ([`src/Basic/ForMathlib/Probability/GaussianPlane.lean`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Probability/GaussianPlane.lean); Mathlib has rotation invariance as `stdGaussian_map`), the `χ²₂` tail and Gaussian interval bounds ([`src/Basic/ForMathlib/Probability/GaussianTail.lean`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Probability/GaussianTail.lean)), and an extended continuous mapping theorem ([`src/Basic/ForMathlib/Probability/Transfer.lean`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Probability/Transfer.lean)). What is general to phylogenetics is already in the library [`lean4-phylogenetics`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics), which keeps what is standard to the field; [`src/Basic/README.md`](../src/Basic/README.md) says what here could still go there.
- **Reuse of [`src/External/`](../src/External).** The rule and Lemma 3 of Allman et al., the forward direction of Huber et al.'s Theorem 5.1 and exchangeability under the NMSC are stated for arbitrary rooted networks, and the quartet metrization of Rhodes for arbitrary unrooted trees. [`src/External/MitchellAllmanRhodes2019/`](../src/External/MitchellAllmanRhodes2019) has the limit law of the likelihood ratio at a cut model. These are for other formalizations citing the same sources. The Splits Equivalence Theorem, which Allman et al. 2023's formalization uses, is in the library ([`Phylogenetics.Tree.SplitsEquivalence`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Tree/SplitsEquivalence.lean)), and the classical bounds and structure theorems for recombination networks went with Gusfield et al. 2007 to [lean4-gusfield2007](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-gusfield2007).

### 4.2 Directions about one paper

- **ECToBlob** ([its report](../papers/ECToBlob/REPORT.md)): Theorem 8.2 for its Cauchy and CBC variants on the boundary, without the hypothesis of positive alternatives (E11); what the algorithm should do on a non-binary network when `T₀` isn't a resolution, where Remark 8.4 fails (E35); duplication and loss on networks, which its Remark 3.4 leaves open; Theorem 8.2 on other laws of non-binary gene trees, for Bon and BBC (E37).

### 4.3 Simpler proofs

Section 11 of each report lists the simpler arguments found while proving. Several of them, in different papers, use one device: an arc `e` induces the split `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` on a quartet exactly when `e` dominates `a₁, a₂` (every root path to them uses `e`) and `b₁, b₂` are not below it, or the reverse ([`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.hasCutSplitOn_iff_separates`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/Cuts.lean#L253)). So a cut edge of a quartet is a statement about each taxon separately. It shortens ECToBlob's Theorem 4.1, Allman et al.'s Lemma 3 and the contradiction in its Theorem 1, and TOB-QMC's Lemma 4, and the converse of Huber et al.'s Theorem 5.1 is proved here this way. Where the formal proofs follow the papers' longer routes, the reports say so. The tests have another: for a trinomial, the Wilks-type limits that the papers cite have an elementary proof from one estimate, `|(1+v) log(1+v) - v - v²/2| ≤ 4|v|³` for `|v| ≤ 1/2` ([`Real.abs_entropyExcess_sub_le`](../src/Basic/ForMathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/EntropyExcess.lean#L50)), which does not use van der Vaart's Theorem 16.7.

## 6. The common objects, and how the formalization reads them

The readings of each paper are in Section 6 of its report. These are the representations that the papers share, most of them from the library [lean4-phylogenetics](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics).

- **Networks** are directed multigraphs with named arcs ([`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/Defs.lean#L30)), rooted, with parallel arcs allowed. The papers' semi-directed networks are handled through the rooted networks they come from; TOB-QMC's `N⁻` is `N` with a root.
- **Unrooted trees** are families of sides of splits ([`Phylogenetics.IsUnrootedTree`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Tree/Defs.lean#L49): [`unroot`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Hierarchy/Defs.lean#L43) of a hierarchy). The tree of blobs `T_B(N)` is the family of nonempty proper sides of cut edges ([`Phylogenetics.treeOfBlobs`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/Quartets.lean#L40)).
  Bridges to the graphs: [`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.blobTree`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/TreeOfBlobs.lean#L127) is the graph with blobs contracted; [`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.mem_treeOfBlobs_iff_blobTree`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/TreeOfBlobs.lean#L213) says the two agree on splits, and [`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.blobTree_isTree`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/TreeOfBlobs.lean#L134) that it is a tree. The root of `blobTree` is not deleted. Allman et al.'s [`AllmanEtAl2023.Trd`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/BlobDefinitions.lean#L422) is the reduced graph, and [`AllmanEtAl2023.Bridges.splits_Trd`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Bridges.lean#L353) says its splits are this family.
- **Induced networks**: `HasCutSplitOn Y A` says that one arc of the subgraph of ancestors of `Y` is the only arc of it crossing a vertex set that meets `Y` in `A`; [`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.hasCutSplitOn_iff_separates`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/Cuts.lean#L253) gives the domination form, and [`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork.hasCutSplitOn_iff_inducedNetwork_hasCutSplit`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Network/Induced.lean#L857) proves it equal to the cut splits of the graph `inducedNetwork` (LSA restriction, then suppression). The later papers use this network, whose CFs are those of `N` on four taxa ([`Phylogenetics.NMSC.nmscCF_inducedNetwork`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Induced.lean#L76)). Allman et al.'s own results use their Definition 6, [`AllmanEtAl2023.induced`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/InducedNetwork.lean#L296), which keeps what lies above the LSA; the two have the same cut-edge splits ([`AllmanEtAl2023.Bridges.induced_hasCutSplit_iff`](../src/AllmanEtAl2023/Bridges.lean#L210)).
- **Quartet trees** are unordered pairs of cherries; [`Phylogenetics.Displays`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Tree/Defs.lean#L84).
- **The NMSC** on arbitrary networks is an order-free sum over histories ([`Phylogenetics.geneTreeDist`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Defs.lean#L94)), the product over vertices and arcs of the joint law of the forests at the bottom and top of each arc. One lineage per taxon. `ρ` is the inheritance correlation of TOB-QMC's supplement, in closed form as an exchangeable Pólya law ([`Phylogenetics.allocationWeight`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Defs.lean#L54), proved equal to the urn in [`Phylogenetics.allocationWeight_eq_urn`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Inheritance.lean#L232)). [`Phylogenetics.sum_geneTreeDist`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Basic.lean#L51) and [`Phylogenetics.geneTreeDist_support`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/NMSC/Basic.lean#L64) validate it: a law on binary rooted trees.
- **Parameters**: `MetricData` has lengths on all arcs and hybridization parameters; `IsMetric` requires positivity on tree edges, and `γ` strictly between 0 and 1 where a reticulation has two parents.
- **Sampling**: `Phylogenetics.iidProb dist n Ev` is the finite sum over samples, proved equal to the product measure ([`Phylogenetics.iidProb_eq_pi`](../src/Basic/Testing/IID.lean#L264)). TOB-QMC's almost-sure statement uses any probability space (`TOBQMC.IsGeneTreeSample`).
- **Test statistics** follow the implementation (MSCquartets, Mitchell et al.). The T1 p-value uses the plug-in `min(1, φ̂)` at all counts; small-count procedures of software are not modelled. Star: likelihood ratio by default, Pearson as `StarTest.pearson`.
- **The T3 test**: [`Phylogenetics.t3Stat`](../src/External/MitchellAllmanRhodes2019/T3Test.lean#L56) is the minimum over topologies of [`t1Stat`](../src/Basic/Testing/Defs.lean#L63), and [`Phylogenetics.t3Var`](../src/External/MitchellAllmanRhodes2019/T3Test.lean#L63) the random variable of Mitchell et al.'s Proposition T3approx, whose squared distance to the three half-lines is [`Phylogenetics.QuartetTests.t3Var_eq_min_sqDistHalfLine`](../src/External/MitchellAllmanRhodes2019/T3Test.lean#L172) (a step they leave as "it can be shown"); at `Z = 0` their formula gives 0, harmless since `Z = 0` has probability zero. The sources differ at the centroid: Mitchell et al.'s null includes it, TINNiK's Fig. 2 caption excludes it, and its anomalous condition `q ≥ 1/3` counts it as anomalous. Lean follows each: [`Phylogenetics.IsT3Null`](../src/External/MitchellAllmanRhodes2019/T3Test.lean#L51) follows Mitchell et al., `TINNiK.NoAnomalousQuartets` TINNiK.
- **Counts of operations** are counts of what the papers' claims are about, tied to the definitions by `Finset.card` of what a step enumerates or by an instrumented run ([`Phylogenetics.NJ.ops_run`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/f1a3592dee6e3f7d5e7a68a0695ac457de23d2de/Phylogenetics/Tree/NeighborJoining.lean#L1976), [`TINNiK.count_bQuartetInference`](../src/TINNiK/Counts.lean#L710)). There is no machine model, so these aren't running times. Linear-time blobs and bridges are [`Phylogenetics.BridgeDFS.isBridge_iff`](../src/Basic/BridgeDFS.lean#L854) and [`Phylogenetics.BridgeDFS.count_eq`](../src/Basic/BridgeDFS.lean#L906), a search by low-points in `|V| + 2 |E|` steps, and [`Phylogenetics.BridgeDFS.mem_treeOfBlobs_iff`](../src/Basic/BridgeDFS.lean#L1222) reads the tree of blobs from it as at most `|V|` intervals of discovery times.
