# Audit of Gusfield et al. 2007

- **Gusfield et al. 2007**: D. Gusfield, V. Bansal, V. Bafna, Y. S. Song, *A decomposition theory for phylogenetic networks and
  incompatible characters*, [J. Comput. Biol. 14 (2007), 1247–1272](https://doi.org/10.1089/cmb.2006.0137), read in the PMC author
  manuscript ([PMC2581772](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2581772/)), and, for its symbols, an [author's copy](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~yss/Pub/decomposition.pdf) on Yun Song's page.

This is the part of [the audit](../REPORT.md) about Gusfield et al. 2007, with the sources that only it uses: Gusfield 1991; Gusfield, Eddhu, Langley 2004; Hudson, Kaplan 1985; Myers, Griffiths 2003; Song, Hein 2005. [The audit](../REPORT.md) has what the five papers have in common: the summary and the status of the formalization, the results from prior work that they cite (Section 2), the literature (Section 10) and what's next (Section 11). The sections here keep its numbers, and the findings are numbered across the five papers: here E42–E54, E67, E76–E79, E85.

## 3. Errors and gaps in the paper

"Line N" here is a line of the plain text of the PMC author manuscript, one line per paragraph ([`docs/CONFIGURATION.md`](../CONFIGURATION.md)). That plain text loses the symbols the page renders as images. An [author's copy on Yun Song's page](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~yss/Pub/decomposition.pdf), made from a DVI file on 2008-01-19, has the symbols and the same words apart from moved floats, footnotes and hyphenation, so it was used to check them. E49, E51 and E54 turn on symbols, and each is in that copy too, so none is an artifact of the PMC text. That copy isn't the journal's typeset text, which wasn't reachable, so whether the journal has them is unknown. The matrices and networks of the figures were compared with the PMC manuscript's images.

Neither Theorem 1, the paper's main result, nor the refutation of the conjectures is affected by anything here. Theorems 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11 and Corollaries 1, 2, 3 hold as printed, under the paper's standing assumption that no column is constant. What fails is Theorem 4 as printed, Theorem 10 if `R(M + S)` is read by line 385's definition (E51), and some sentences around them.

**E42. Theorem 4: the definition of "efficient" omits the node where a blob is entered (an error).** Lines 326–328: "we say that a node v in blob b is an external node if there is an edge from v to some node outside of b. We say a phylogenetic network is efficient if [it] does not contain two external nodes with the same node labels, and for each external node v (in a blob b), there is exactly one edge from v to a node off of b." Theorem 4 (lines 334–336): for "any efficient fully-decomposed network for M", contracting the blobs gives a tree that "is necessarily the tree T̄".

The definition says nothing about the edge entering a blob, and neither does the proof. An exit can sit at the entry node of a blob while rows lie above the blob. Then line 342, "in the other subnetwork, no leaf label contains S_v(C)", is false: the rows above the blob contain the super-character of its top. Line 341, "each super-character derived from C labels exactly one external node", needs the entry node too: in Figure 2 made efficient, the super-character `00` of the component `{2, 5}` labels no external node ([`Gusfield2007.line341_false_fig2`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2080)). There `T′` is `T̄`, so that one is a gap in the proof, not a further failure of the theorem.

The counterexample is [`Gusfield2007.topRows`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Examples.lean#L100), with rows `00000, 01000, 10000, 11001, 11011, 11101, 11111`. The network has a blob on `00000, 01000, 10000, 11000` with the sites 1, 2; an arc with the site 5 from `11000` to `11001`; a blob on `11001, 11011, 11101, 11111` with the sites 3, 4; and a pendant arc at each node of the two blobs other than `11000`. It is efficient in the printed sense and fully decomposed, in any crossover model, with no recombination leaf, and with a site on every arc that has both ends off the blobs and isn't pendant. The split `{00000, 01000, 10000, 11001} | {11011, 11101, 11111}` of `T̄` is missing from `T′`: [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_false_top`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1943). The split is the witness to quote, since the number of edges depends on how line 264 is read. Moving the row `11001` up to a new node on the entering arc gives another efficient fully decomposed network, whose `T′` is `T̄`, so `T′` isn't invariant, against line 346: [`Gusfield2007.topRows_not_invariant`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1952). A smaller instance, with one blob and a root of degree 3, is on `{0000, 0001, 0010, 0110, 1010, 1110}`: [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_false_small`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1974).

The result stands in the following form. With the entry node counted, the theorem holds up to the two conventions of E44 and the site-free arcs of E43: for a fully decomposed network, `T′` is `T̄` exactly when the network has [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsEfficientAtEntry`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1721), [`Gusfield2007.Network.RegularArcs`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L456) and [`Gusfield2007.Network.DegreeCond`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L467) ([`Gusfield2007.theorem4_entry`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1843)). That definition reads "external" as having an edge to or from a node outside the blob, compares labels on one blob at a time, and counts the entering edge among the edges of its node. Lines 341 and 342 are then true: [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsEfficientAtEntry.existsUnique_port`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1803), [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsEfficientAtEntry.line342Claim`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1798). With the printed definition, [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_iff`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1699) is exact: `T′` is `T̄` exactly when the network is efficient and has [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsTreeNormalStructural`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L888) and the degree condition. [`Gusfield2007.theorem4`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1310) and [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_splits`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L929) are its forward direction. These hypotheses can be met: for every `M` with at least three rows there is such a network, with an interior root ([`Gusfield2007.exists_realization`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Realizability.lean#L327)). So `T̄` is the invariant structure of the networks the paper has in mind, and we read the omission as a slip in a definition: the proof's own line 341 needs the entry node. The use of Theorem 4 at line 418 is E53.

This repair is in print, and it isn't ours. Gusfield's book *ReCombinatorics* (MIT Press, 2014), Section 7.3.5, says "A node v in a blob B is an external node of B if v is adjacent (without regard to edge direction) to some node v′ not in B". It calls an ARG canonical if "No blob contains two nodes labeled with the same sequence", "Each external node v (in a blob B) is incident with exactly one cut edge", "If the root node v of a blob B is an external node, then the cut edge incident with v is directed into v", and "Every blob contains at least one incompatible pair of sites". Its Theorem 7.3.3 says "All canonical, fully decomposed ARGs for M have the same undirected backbone tree", and its Corollary 7.3.4 is line 341 repaired: the number of external nodes of a blob "is exactly the number of distinct sequences in M(C)". We read the book only through short snippets from Open Library's public search inside, and its table of contents, and not its proofs; someone should read Sections 7.3.5 and 7.4 in print. The snippets show no statement that the 2007 definition fails, and the section's introduction says "(with some technical qualification)". What the Lean adds is the failure under the printed definition, by explicit networks ([`Gusfield2007.theorem4_false_top`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1943), [`Gusfield2007.topRows_not_invariant`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1952)); the exact characterization in both directions, where the snippets show one direction, for `IsEfficientAtEntry`, which is weaker than canonical (a canonical ARG is efficient at the entry, but not conversely: a blob can repeat a label away from its external nodes, and [`Gusfield2007.fourRootNet`](../../src/Gusfield2007/CounterexampleNets.lean#L439) has an exit at the root of its blob, against the third condition); and the check in the kernel.

**E43. Theorem 4: site-free arcs (a case line 97 allows and the statement must exclude).** Line 97: "There may be additional edges that are assigned no integers." The proof at lines 341–344 looks only at edges leaving a blob, and never meets such an arc with both ends off the blobs, on which the theorem fails as printed. Take the rows `10000, 01000, 00100, 00010, 00001`, with arcs from the root to the first two and a site-free arc to a node with the other three: a perfect phylogeny, so efficient and fully decomposed under either definition, with no blob at all, and with every node of `T′` of degree at most two carrying a leaf. `T̄` is a star, and `T′` has one more edge: [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_false_siteFree`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2028), [`Gusfield2007.fiveNet_split`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2045). Counting the entry node doesn't help, since there is no blob. The theorem holds when no arc with both ends off the blobs, other than a pendant one, is site-free: the field `arc_labelled` of [`Gusfield2007.Network.RegularArcs`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L456). [`theorem4_entry`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1843) shows that this is necessary. Contracting such arcs repairs a given network, but the paper doesn't say to. Whether the ARGs of Gusfield's 2014 book (E42) allow such arcs wasn't visible in the snippets we read.

**E44. Theorem 4: two conventions (implicit, not errors).** Read literally, the printed statement also fails on a root of degree two and on a recombination leaf. For `M = {0, 1}`, the network with a root and two leaves has a `T′` with three nodes, where `T̄` has two ([`Gusfield2007.theorem4Printed_false_rootDegreeTwo`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L439)); line 418 itself places a root on an edge, so read `T′` with the root suppressed. For `M = {00, 01, 10, 11}`, with `11` a recombination leaf, `T′` has three edges and `T̄` four ([`Gusfield2007.theorem4Printed_false_recombLeaf`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1920)); Figure 1's caption speaks of "the recombinant sequence exiting a recombination node", and line 341 needs it, so give a recombination node a pendant arc. We don't count these as errors. They are [`Gusfield2007.Network.DegreeCond`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L467) and the field `recomb_not_leaf` of [`Gusfield2007.Network.RegularArcs`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L456).

**E45. §4.2, line 373 (an error).** "With the above definition of 'treeness', it is clear that a phylogenetic network is 'the most tree-like' if and only if T̄ is the resulting undirected tree". Treeness is the number of edges of `T′`, among networks whose blobs share no node and with no node of in-degree and out-degree one (lines 364–368). Site-free arcs add edges: on [`Gusfield2007.starRows`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Examples.lean#L105), the star with four edges has `T′ = T̄`, and resolving it with two site-free arcs gives six ([`Gusfield2007.mostTreeLikePrinted_false`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2137)). A reading that doesn't count a site-free arc with both ends off the blobs escapes that example, but not the next two. They are efficient and fully decomposed, with no such arc: their site-free arcs that aren't pendant are at the blob, from the root to its top, and in the second also from its top to a node with two rows. On the rows `0000 0001 0010 0100 1000 1100` the treeness is 7, the number of edges of `T̄`, yet `T′` isn't `T̄` ([`Gusfield2007.treeness_example₁`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2151)). On `00000 00001 00010 00100 01000 10000 11000` it is 9, more than the 8 edges of `T̄` ([`Gusfield2007.treeness_example₂`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2173)). Two narrower counts aren't in the paper, and we computed them by hand on these networks, not in Lean. Counting only the edges with a site doesn't escape them: the counts tie, at 2 = 2 and 3 = 3, while `T′` isn't `T̄`. Counting only the edges that carry a character of `B`, as line 264 labels `T̄`, does: 5 < 6 and 6 < 7. So these two examples refute the definition as printed, and that last reading isn't refuted by them, though on [`starRows`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Examples.lean#L105) it ties at 4 = 4 unless the site-free arcs are also contracted. Line 373 is a remark, and no theorem of the paper rests on it. Theorem 7.4.1 of Gusfield's 2014 book (E42) restates it for canonical ARGs ("the most treelike"); we didn't see that book's definition of treelike, so these examples aren't compared with it. What's true is [`Gusfield2007.treeness_le_and_eq_iff`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2333): for networks with node-disjoint blobs, each with the sites of one component, and with `RegularArcs`, [`Gusfield2007.Network.NonPendantPortsDistinct`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2255) and the degree condition, the treeness is at most the number of edges of `T̄`, with equality exactly when `T′` is `T̄`. These needn't be fully decomposed or efficient ([`Gusfield2007.treeness_nonFD_example`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2354)), and the inequality can be strict ([`Gusfield2007.treeness_strict_example`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2390)). The sentence of line 375 that all of a component lies on one blob is right. Blobs that merge components are E67.

**E67. Gusfield et al. line 373, with blobs that merge components (an error; no theorem rests on it).** Line 373's characterization is stated for all networks whose blobs share no node, and a blob can hold sites of two components. Then the inequality can fail: [`Gusfield2007.mergedNet`](../../src/Gusfield2007/MergedTreeness.lean#L29) derives the compatible rows `1000, 0100, 0010, 0001`, in any crossover model, with every condition of the true form of E45 except that its one blob has the sites 0 and 1, and its treeness is 5, while `T̄` has 4 edges ([`Gusfield2007.treeness_merged_false`](../../src/Gusfield2007/MergedTreeness.lean#L94)). The equality can also fail to say what it should: [`Gusfield2007.tieNet`](../../src/Gusfield2007/MergedTreeness.lean#L882), whose blob merges a non-trivial component with an isolated site, has treeness 7, the number of edges of `T̄`, while `T′` isn't `T̄` ([`Gusfield2007.treeness_merged_tie`](../../src/Gusfield2007/MergedTreeness.lean#L978)). What's true: the inequality holds when each blob has a site of a non-trivial component or at most one site ([`Gusfield2007.treeness_le_of_merged`](../../src/Gusfield2007/MergedTreeness.lean#L740)), and the inequality with the "if and only if" holds when each blob has the sites of one component or none ([`Gusfield2007.treeness_le_and_eq_iff_of_siteFree`](../../src/Gusfield2007/MergedTreeness.lean#L192)). A blob with at least two sites, all isolated in `G(M)`, is the case that breaks the inequality.

**E46. Line 156 (a case line 97 allows and the statement must exclude).** "The undirected tree created by ignoring the directions of the edges of T is invariant over all perfect phylogenies for M. Similarly, there is a unique phylogenetic network that derives M, with ancestral sequence S and without recombination cycles". The paper's networks may have site-free arcs (line 97), and with them neither holds: [`Gusfield2007.fiveNet`](../../src/Gusfield2007/CounterexampleNets.lean#L494) and [`Gusfield2007.fiveStarNet`](../../src/Gusfield2007/CounterexampleNets.lean#L500) derive one matrix with no recombination, and their trees have six and five edges ([`Gusfield2007.perfectPhylogeny_not_invariant`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2480)). The classical result that line 156 recalls can only be about trees without such arcs; the expositions it points to weren't read. Formalized: [`Gusfield2007.perfectPhylogeny_invariant`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2465), under `RegularArcs` and the degree condition, and [`Gusfield1991.network_rooted_unique`](../../src/External/Gusfield1991/PerfectPhylogeny.lean#L885), among the reduced trees [`Gusfield1991.IsReducedTree`](../../src/External/Gusfield1991/PerfectPhylogeny.lean#L230).

**E47. Line 180: the paraphrase of a cited definition is too weak (an error of wording).** "In the case of galled-trees, every reduced galled-tree for M must be fully-decomposed. A galled-tree is 'reduced' if every recombination cycle contains some incompatible sites." With this wording the claim fails: on the rows `000, 101, 010, 110` there is a single-crossover galled tree with one cycle, holding two incompatible sites and also a compatible one, which isn't on a tree edge ([`GusfieldEtAl2004.line180_printed_false`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/GalledTree.lean#L573)). The source's Definition 2.2 says more: "every gall contains at least one conflicted pair of sites, and contains no unconflicted sites". With it the claim holds, in the source's rooted form and in the unrooted form of 2007: [`GusfieldEtAl2004.IsReducedGalledTree.isFullyDecomposedFor_conflictGraph`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/GalledTree.lean#L502), [`GusfieldEtAl2004.IsReducedGalledTree.isFullyDecomposedFor_incompatGraph`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/GalledTree.lean#L497). The cited theorem stands, and no proof of the paper uses this sentence.

**E48. Lines 403 and 425: a step that fails on one `M` (an error; no result affected).** Line 403: "the rooted conjectures imply the original unrooted conjectures, by letting S be the ancestral sequence that appears in the optimal phylogenetic network using R(M) (respectively R¹(M)) recombination nodes." An ancestral sequence can merge components: the network that S-min decomposability gives is fully decomposed for `G_S(M)`, not for `G(M)`. On Figure 6, Figure 7's topology with the root relabelled `001100` gives `R¹_S(M) = 3 = R¹(M)` with `G_S(M)` connected, so `M` is S-min-1 decomposable at that `S`; yet `F¹(M) = 4`, so `M` isn't min-1 decomposable ([`Gusfield2007.fig6_hG_necessary`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Counterexamples.lean#L252); the example isn't in the paper). Formalized: [`Gusfield2007.minDecomposable_of_sMinDecomposable`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Conjectures.lean#L110), with the hypothesis that `G_S(M)` has the components of `G(M)`. As an implication between the conjectures, the sentence holds only because the rooted conjectures are false ([`Gusfield2007.rootedConjecture_imp_unrootedConjecture`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Counterexamples.lean#L554), Section 7). The other direction fails too, even with `S` a row, where `G_S(M) = G(M)`: [`Gusfield2007.printedRows`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L519) is min-1 decomposable ([`Gusfield2007.minDecomposable_printedRows`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L673)) and not S-min-1 decomposable at its own row [`Gusfield2007.printedRoot`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L526) ([`Gusfield2007.theorem10Printed_single_false`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L681)). That `S` isn't an optimal root, so this isn't an instance of line 403's step; it says that neither notion gives the other on one `M`. Line 425, "when M is S-min (respectively S-min-1) decomposable for all S, then M is min-decomposable", is a stronger hypothesis on one `M`, and it stays open: [`Gusfield2007.MinDecomposableOfForallS`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Conjectures.lean#L118) is a definition, neither proved nor refuted. It holds when `H(M) = R(M)` ([`Gusfield2007.minDecomposableOfForallS_of_haplotypeBound_eq`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L79)). Line 590 uses the step: "which also implies that the rooted versions are false" is the paper's only argument for the rooted multiple-crossover case. That conclusion holds directly, because the ancestral sequence of Figure 9 is a row of Figure 8, where `G_S(M) = G(M)` ([`Gusfield2007.rootedConjecture_multiple_false`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Counterexamples.lean#L479)). The single-crossover case is shown directly at line 608 ([`Gusfield2007.rootedConjecture_single_false`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Counterexamples.lean#L224)). The positive results of §5.2 are rooted, and don't use the step.

**E49. Lines 554–555 (false as printed; probably `M` for `M + S`, as at line 552).** "It is easy to prove that for any non-degenerate galled-network [N] for M, a pair of sites is incompatible in L if and only if it is incompatible in M, and therefore G(L) and G(M) are identical." The root's sequence can supply a fourth pair. On [`Gusfield2007.galledNet`](../../src/Gusfield2007/GalledNetworks.lean#L223), with root `0000` and `M = {1000, 1010, 0100, 0101, 1100}`, the sites 1 and 2 are compatible in `M` and incompatible in `L` ([`Gusfield2007.galledIncompatPrinted_false`](../../src/Gusfield2007/GalledNetworks.lean#L311)). True with `M + S`: [`Gusfield2007.incompatible_labelSet_iff`](../../src/Gusfield2007/GalledNetworks.lean#L177), so `G_S(L) = G_S(M)` ([`Gusfield2007.conflictGraph_labelSet_eq`](../../src/Gusfield2007/GalledNetworks.lean#L192)). That is what the application of Theorem 5 needs, and it is proved: [`Gusfield2007.sMinDecomposable_of_galled`](../../src/Gusfield2007/GalledNetworks.lean#L200). The printed form holds when `S ∈ M`: [`Gusfield2007.incompatible_labelSet_iff_of_mem`](../../src/Gusfield2007/GalledNetworks.lean#L207). Huson and Klöpper 2007 wasn't read (Section 2), so this finding rests on the definition of non-degenerate in their errata, and the definition of galled in Huson et al. 2009; the 2007 wording may differ.

**E50. Line 560 (an error).** From the haplotype bound "it follows that" equality holds "only when every node in [the network] is labeled by a sequence in M + S. Hence, when the haplotype bound is tight for the appropriate crossover model, every node in [the network] must be visible" (lines 560–564). This fails on identical columns. For `M = {00, 11}` and `S = 00`, `H = 2 - 1 - 1 = 0`, and [`Gusfield2007.chainNet`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L448), the path `00 → 10 → 11` with a leaf `00` at the root, has no recombination and an invisible node: [`Gusfield2007.visibleOfTightPrinted_false`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L525). Line 580's count allows for this: the labelled edges are at least the distinct sites. With distinct columns the sentence holds: [`MyersGriffiths2003.allVisible_of_eq`](../../src/External/MyersGriffiths2003/Bound.lean#L97). For Theorem 6 this does no harm, since its hypotheses give distinct columns. For Theorem 10 it needs another step, and the theorem holds: see E51.

**E51. Theorem 10: false if `R(M + S)` is read by line 385's definition (proved for single crossovers, conditional for multiple); lines 525 and 560 use the symbol for a network with ancestral sequence `S`, so probably a slip for `R_S(M)`, under which it is proved.** Line 568: "if H(M + S) = R¹(M + S) then M is S-min-1 decomposable, and if H(M + S) = R(M + S) then M is S-min decomposable". Line 385 defines `R(M)` as the minimum over all networks for `M`, and `R_S(M)` as the minimum over those with ancestral sequence `S`, so read literally `R(M + S)` is a minimum over all ancestral sequences. The paper uses the symbol otherwise. Line 525 has a network "for M, with ancestral sequence S, using R¹(M + S) single-crossover recombinations", and lines 559–562 take "an optimal phylogenetic network for M, with ancestral sequence S" and write its count as `R¹(M + S)` "(or R(M + S))". Both are in the author's copy too. The proof of Theorem 10 is that passage, so what it reasons on is a network with `R_S(M)` nodes. Read literally, the two counts can differ under the hypothesis, and then a tight network has another root and the proof doesn't apply.

With the literal reading, the statement fails for single crossovers: [`Gusfield2007.theorem10Printed_single_false`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L681). On [`Gusfield2007.printedRows`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L519), 14 rows on 8 sites, with `S` the row [`Gusfield2007.printedRoot`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L526) and all columns distinct, `H(M + S) = R¹(M + S) = 5` and `R¹_S(M) = 6`, while the two components of `G_S(M)` need at least 4 and 3 for their sequences at `S`, so at least 7 ([`Gusfield2007.seven_le_sum_printed`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L659)). All of this is checked by the kernel.

With the same reading, for multiple crossovers it is false given one computation: [`Gusfield2007.theorem10Printed_multiple_false_of_search`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Theorem10PrintedMultiple.lean#L294), on [`Gusfield2007.printedMRows`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Theorem10PrintedMultiple.lean#L51), 74 rows on 34 sites, with `S` a row and all columns distinct, where `H(M + S) = R(M + S) = 39`. The hypothesis, [`Gusfield2007.printedMSearch`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Theorem10PrintedMultiple.lean#L269), is a finite search: that two extra labels never suffice on the second of the two components. Given it, that component needs at least 39 and the first at least 4, against `R_S(M) ≤ 42`. Lean's kernel hasn't completed the search. Outside the kernel, it is supported by two separate programs by the same author, not in the repository, and by Lean's `#eval` of the function in the hypothesis, which runs interpreted code. None of these is a proof, so the theorem is conditional, as its statement says, and nothing else depends on it.

With `R_S(M)`, the reading that the paper's own use suggests, the statement is true, and it is proved in every crossover model with no hypothesis on the columns: [`Gusfield2007.theorem10`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L680). That is the form that Theorem 6 uses, so no result of the paper is affected. Since line 560 is false for identical columns (E50), Lean replaces visibility by the step that on two sites with different columns, the labels of a tight network are a pair occurring in `M + S` ([`Gusfield2007.exists_row_of_eq_haplotypeBound`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L610)), which is what the proof of Corollary 2 uses from visibility. The paper's own proof, for distinct columns, is [`Gusfield2007.sMinDecomposable_of_haplotype_tight`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L565). The literal form holds when a tight root differs from `S` in at most one component, given distinct columns ([`Gusfield2007.sMinDecomposable_of_tight_root`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L422)). And `H(M) = R(M)` gives min decomposability ([`Gusfield2007.minDecomposable_of_haplotypeBound_eq`](../../src/Gusfield2007/OpenStatements.lean#L33)), which isn't in the paper.

**E52. Lines 329–330 (a gap).** "If [the network] is not efficient, it can be easily modified to become efficient without increasing the number of recombinations used. Also, if [it] is fully-decomposed, then the efficient network derived from it is also fully-decomposed." The paper gives no modification, and the obvious one hides a case: moving the exits of a node to another node with its label can leave a recombination node as a new leaf. That node is then wasted, and removing it from a fully decomposed network takes Lemma 5 and Theorem 3. The statement holds for the printed definition: [`Gusfield2007.exists_isEfficient`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L260), by induction on recombination nodes and then exits, and [`Gusfield2007.exists_isEfficient_of_any`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L285) for any network. Gusfield's 2014 book (E42) gives a modification in the proof of its Theorem 7.3.2, of which the snippets show only the start. With the entry node counted the statement holds too, for both `G(M)` and `G_S(M)`: [`Gusfield2007.exists_isEfficientAtEntry_of_fullyDecomposed`](../../src/Gusfield2007/EntryNet.lean#L516), and [`Gusfield2007.exists_isEfficientAtEntry_of_any`](../../src/Gusfield2007/EntryNet.lean#L551) for any network. The network then needn't have `T′ = T̄`, which is a matter of tree arcs in the networks at its central nodes: [`Gusfield2007.exists_realization`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Realizability.lean#L327) is about `T′`, and this about the count.

**E53. Gaps in the proofs (all the results hold).**

- **Line 135.** That a cycle sharing no edge can be made node-disjoint needs that a node shared by blobs has arcs in from at most one of them, which is unstated: two arcs into a node lie on one cycle. [`Gusfield2007.Network.exists_isCycle_of_tgt_eq`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Blobs.lean#L344), [`Gusfield2007.Network.exists_blobsNodeDisjoint`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Blobs.lean#L1180).
- **Line 141.** If the contracted graph had a cycle, "that cycle would correspond to a recombination cycle in" the network. A cycle of the underlying graph isn't a recombination cycle: it can change direction several times. Each of its arcs is on one, since all nodes are reachable from the root: [`Gusfield2007.Network.isTreeArc_iff_isBridge`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Blobs.lean#L397), [`Gusfield2007.Network.blobbedTree_isTree`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Blobs.lean#L465).
- **Lines 187–190.** The rooted analogue "follows from Theorem 1". The statement of Theorem 1 for `M + S` puts `S` at a leaf, and a network can't be re-rooted through a blob. What gives the result is the proof of Theorem 1, as line 312 says for Theorem 3: [`Gusfield2007.theorem1_rooted`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Decomposition.lean#L291).
- **Line 264.** "Applying the Perfect Phylogeny Theorem", there is a tree "where each character of B labels one edge in T̄, and each edge is labeled by one or more characters of B". That theorem gives a tree with possible unlabelled edges and nodes of degree two, and "each edge" can't hold with rows at leaves on `{00, 01, 10}`. Line 289 says "every internal edge", which is how [`Gusfield2007.IsTBar`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L433) reads it; [`Gusfield2007.IsPerfectPhylogeny.exists_normal`](../../src/Gusfield2007/TreeContract.lean#L321) contracts what isn't needed.
- **Line 271.** "From the facts that all columns in B are distinct" is false: a trivial component gives two columns with one bipartition, as line 245 says, and columns from different components can have one bipartition, as in [`topRows`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Examples.lean#L100), where the column of super-character `11` and that of the site 5 coincide. What's used is that the splits of edges are distinct: [`Gusfield2007.IsTBar`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L433).
- **Line 295.** "The three other cases are similar and omitted." The two where `e₁` has a non-dominant super-character take another argument: all the rows beyond `e` have the dominant super-character, which must be that of `e₂`, so that `e` and `e₂` would have one split ([`Gusfield2007.IsTBar.edges_incident`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SuperCharacters.lean#L392)).
- **Line 299.** The sentence on connecting edges uses what line 277 declines to assert ([`Gusfield2007.adj_center_labelled`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SuperCharacters.lean#L750)), and "each central-star node" uses that distinct non-trivial components have distinct centres ([`Gusfield2007.IsTBar.center_ne`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SuperCharacters.lean#L780)). When the root is a central node `v_C`, its label is 0 on every character of `B` from `C`, by Lemma 4, so by our reading it gives no sequence on the sites of `C`, and `S(C)` takes a choice. This observation is ours: line 416 says of "the situation in the proof of Theorem 1" that "the label on v can be used for the ancestral sequence of bv". Theorem 1's construction in Lean takes the root at a row, one of the choices that line 299 leaves arbitrary. With the root at any other node of `T̄`, [`Gusfield2007.exists_network_rooted_at`](../../src/Gusfield2007/RootedFrame.lean#L958) takes any sequence on `C` for the central root, and has `T′ = T̄` and all the hypotheses of [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_entry`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1843). A network deriving all the rows can't have `T′ = T̄` with its root at a row ([`Gusfield2007.root_not_mem_range`](../../src/Gusfield2007/RootedFrame.lean#L1028)), so there the row has no leaf ([`Gusfield2007.exists_network_rooted_at_row`](../../src/Gusfield2007/RootedFrame.lean#L994)).
- **Lines 312–316, Theorem 3.** "Establishes" (line 312) omits three steps: if `S(C) ∈ M(C)`, a leaf labelled `S(C)` has no edge of `T̄` to carry ([`Gusfield2007.RS_erase_eq`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Decomposition.lean#L300)); an optimal network has no site-free blob ([`Gusfield2007.Decomp.exists_copy_of_siteFree`](../../src/Gusfield2007/DecompNet.lean#L462)); and if `S ∈ M`, the row needs its own leaf below the root ([`Gusfield2007.Decomp.addLeaf`](../../src/Gusfield2007/DecompNet.lean#L241)).
- **Lines 341–342.** See E42.
- **Line 411.** That every `M` is min decomposable for recombinations modelling mutations "follows from results on the maximum parsimony problem using median networks, as established by Bandelt et al. (1995)". Those results are there: "all most parsimonious trees for a set V of 0-1 vectors can be realized in the median network" (p. 745), and at a cut node of that network the characters split into two sets, and the most parsimonious trees "are exactly the trees composed by the MP trees" of the two parts (p. 747; "decomposition along cut nodes", p. 748). Neither paper writes the link from cut nodes to the components of `G(M)`, which the statement needs; for Buneman graphs it appeared later, as Theorem 5.1 of Dress et al. 2011 (Section 10). The statement holds: [`Gusfield2007.minDecomposable_pointwise`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L168), by another route, which isn't forced (Section 7).
- **Line 418.** "The correctness of the procedure follows from Theorem 4 and the fact that placing the root of T̄ at a leaf or on the interior of an edge will not reduce the number of required recombinations". Theorem 4 is false as printed, and an optimal fully decomposed network needn't satisfy its corrected form. The conclusion holds: [`Gusfield2007.F_eq_sInf_interior`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Conjectures.lean#L696), and the second fact is [`Gusfield2007.exists_interior_sum_rootCost_le`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Conjectures.lean#L651) (Section 7).
- **Line 576.** The two conditions of the modification don't exclude a recombination node labelled `S`, and the count is then off by one ([`Gusfield2007.exists_reattach_of_card_lt`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L712)). And "does not label any other node" asks too much: a node reached by a site-free arc can share the label. Only recombination nodes need to move: [`Gusfield2007.exists_tight_of_allVisible`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L764).

### Points on the sources that only this paper uses

These are about the sources that the five papers cite, and none affects a result of the five papers. Section 2 has the earlier points on them.

**E76. Gusfield, Eddhu, Langley 2004, Corollary 2.1 (an error in one direction, which the source also uses; it needs every non-coalescent node to branch).** "A sequence is in M(C) − 0 if and only if it is the sequence S_v(C) for some node v ≠ w on Q. Stated differently, the node labels of the non-coalescent nodes on Q, restricted to sites in C, are exactly the sequences in M(C) − 0." A node of a gall with no edge off it has a label that needn't be a row: [`GusfieldEtAl2004.corollary2_1_printed_false`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/GalledTree.lean#L593), with the rows `000, 101, 010, 110`, under the source's standing assumptions. The source's proof shows only that each row is the label of a node on the gall ([`GusfieldEtAl2004.exists_mem_label_eq`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/GalledTree.lean#L272)). The other direction holds at a branching node, by its Lemma 2.1 ([`GusfieldEtAl2004.label_tgt_of_notMem`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/GalledTree.lean#L253)), which is how the sufficiency in its Theorem 2.2 uses it, "with the specified branching edges". Its Section 3 uses that direction with no such hypothesis: it adds a leaf at each non-coalescent node, labelled by that node, and says "by Corollary 2.1, the leaf labels ... are exactly the sequences in M(C), other than" the all-0 one. The algorithm in Lean doesn't rest on that. Gusfield et al. 2007 don't use the corollary.

**E77. Gusfield, Eddhu, Langley 2004: gaps (all the results hold).**

- **Theorem 2.2, the general fact.** That two sites in conflict lie on a recombination cycle in any network is proved by removing the other sites, contracting edges and deleting one arc into each recombination node. That an arc can be deleted so that the leaves keep their labels has no proof, and it can't be done at a node that takes one site from each parent when these differ at both sites. The statement holds: [`GusfieldEtAl2004.exists_isCycle_of_conflict`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/SameBlob.lean#L359), by tracing states up, with no surgery.
- **Theorem 2.7.** A gall with only unconflicted sites is replaced by a perfect phylogeny of "the sequences labeling the nodes on Q". Those can conflict when no row shows it: a gall whose only leaf is below the recombination node. The statement holds: [`GusfieldEtAl2004.theorem2_7`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/ReducedExists.lean#L582), [`GusfieldEtAl2004.theorem2_7_moreover`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/Optimality.lean#L160).
- **Lemma 8.5.** "Otherwise, v would be a branching node": a node on a gall needn't be branching, since an edge with no site above a node with no edge off the gall can be removed. The claim about the conflict graph holds: [`GusfieldEtAl2004.lemma8_5`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/Optimality.lean#L382).
- **Lemma 8.6.** It applies Corollary 5.1 after a removal, and that corollary's proof uses distinct columns, which a removal doesn't keep: on the rows `100, 110, 001, 111`, with a reduced galled tree with one gall, removing `100` makes columns 1 and 2 identical. The statement holds on a fixed galled tree: [`GusfieldEtAl2004.lemma8_6`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/Optimality.lean#L441), [`GusfieldEtAl2004.theorem8_2`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/Optimality.lean#L604) (Section 7).
- **`m ≤ 2n`.** The source's `O(nm + n³)` rests on this bound, cited from Wang et al. 2001, which we didn't read and didn't prove. The bound here, [`GusfieldEtAl2004.Algorithm.galledBuildOps_le`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/BuildCount.lean#L407), is in `n` and `m`, and isn't the source's.

**E78. Myers, Griffiths 2003, p. 393 (a misprint).** In Appendix A, "by setting r_p or r_q, respectively, to be large enough" must be `r_p` or `r_{q-1}`: a constraint from `p` to `q` has no term in `r_q`. The result holds: [`MyersGriffiths2003.exists_feasible_extend`](../../src/External/MyersGriffiths2003/Algorithm1.lean#L370).

**E79. Hudson, Kaplan 1985, Appendix 2 (a gap).** "To each interval we must assign at least one recombination event. Hence, R_M equals the final number of intervals on the list." That gives one inequality. The other needs that the final list's events also serve the deleted intervals, which isn't said. The result holds: [`HudsonKaplan1985.exists_pierce`](../../src/External/HudsonKaplan1985/Algorithm.lean#L348), [`HudsonKaplan1985.hudsonKaplan_isOptimalValue`](../../src/External/HudsonKaplan1985/Algorithm.lean#L452).

**E85. Klöpper's thesis, pp. 86–87, Figure 8.1 (unchecked).** Its caption says that the figure is "a counter-example to the Decomposition Theorem in the case where we only minimize the number of reticulations and the number of edges (as defined in [HK07])". This bears on what "minimal" means in the conjecture of line 590, and it wasn't checked here. [`Gusfield2007.Conjecture22`](../../src/Gusfield2007/HusonKloepper.lean#L801) keeps only the first criterion, the number of reticulations, so it asks less of the network.

## 6. How the formalization reads the paper

- **Sites and rows.** Sites are a linearly ordered type, a row is a function to `Bool`, and `M` is a finite set of rows, since the paper assumes no duplicate rows (line 112). `M(C)` is the set of distinct restrictions, as the super-characters of line 245 are. The standing assumption that no column is constant is [`Gusfield2007.NoConstantColumn`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L74), a hypothesis of the results that use it.
- **Networks** ([`Gusfield2007.Network`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L129)) have no parallel arcs. [`Gusfield2007.Network.Derives`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L180) is a bijection from the leaves to the rows, since the network has "exactly n" leaves. A recombination node may be a leaf, as nothing at line 94 excludes it; this is what E44 is about. There is no bridge from these networks, which carry sites and sequences, to the rooted networks of the other four papers, and the modules reach only Mathlib and their sources in [`src/External/`](../../src/External).
- **Crossovers.** A single crossover is a prefix of one parent followed by a suffix of the other, the prefix being a lower set, possibly empty or everything; a recombination that copies a parent can be dropped, so no minimum changes. `Gusfield2007.Crossover.pointwise` is a third model, the recombinations of Section 4.1 that model one back or recurrent mutation, which Corollary 3 needs: "S-min decomposable" can't mean multiple crossovers there, since Figure 8 refutes that.
- **Blobs.** A blob is the arc set of a class of recombination cycles under sharing an arc. "No blob shares a node with any other blob" (lines 135, 140) is [`Gusfield2007.Network.BlobsNodeDisjoint`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L224), a conjunct of [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsFullyDecomposedFor`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L257); [`Gusfield2007.Network.exists_blobsNodeDisjoint`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Blobs.lean#L1180) shows that no count depends on the convention. `T′` is the quotient of the nodes by the components of the arcs on cycles, a tree with no assumption on blobs ([`Gusfield2007.Network.blobbedTree_isTree`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Blobs.lean#L465)).
- **`T̄`** ([`Gusfield2007.IsTBar`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L433)) has every row at a leaf, every internal edge labelled, and no node of degree two, as lines 271–289 use it. Line 264's "each edge is labeled" can't hold with rows at leaves. It agrees with the split families of the library, which contain the singletons. With line 264's reading, `T̄` of [`topRows`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Examples.lean#L100) has eight edges, which is why E42 quotes the split.
- **Theorem 4's form.** [`Gusfield2007.Theorem4Form`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L49) asks for an isomorphism of graphs from `T′` to `T̄` that respects the rows at the leaves, which is what "is necessarily the tree" means.
- **`R`, `F`.** [`Gusfield2007.R`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L291), [`Gusfield2007.RS`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L295), [`Gusfield2007.F`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L300), [`Gusfield2007.FS`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L305) are minima over networks on finite types, in `ℕ`. `sInf ∅ = 0` occurs only on an empty `M`, which is excluded. The haplotype bound [`Gusfield2007.haplotypeBound`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L114) is an integer, as printed, and is negative on `{10, 01}`. `d_oSPR` is in `ℕ∞`, as unreachable pairs would otherwise cost 0.
- **Trivial components.** Lean keeps both columns of `B` that a trivial component gives, where lines 245 and 281 drop one, so the second clause of Lemma 4 is for non-trivial components ([`Gusfield2007.lemma4`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SuperCharacters.lean#L672)).
- **Theorem 10** is [`Gusfield2007.theorem10`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L680) for the rooted count `R_S(M)`, in each crossover model and with no hypothesis on the columns. The paper prints `R(M + S)`, and lines 525 and 560 use that symbol for a network with ancestral sequence `S`, so we read it as probably a slip for `R_S(M)`. [`Gusfield2007.Theorem10Printed`](../../src/Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L561) is the statement with `R` read by line 385's definition, as a definition, with its refutations (E51).
- **The conjecture of Huson and Klöpper.** [`Gusfield2007.Conjecture22`](../../src/Gusfield2007/HusonKloepper.lean#L801) is the statement in Klöpper's thesis, on its networks ([`Gusfield2007.ReticulateNetwork`](../../src/Gusfield2007/HusonKloepper.lean#L474), its Definition 2.1). "Minimal" keeps only the first of the thesis's criteria, the number of reticulations. `Σ(e)` isn't spelled out in the part of the thesis we read, and a split is in it when it is the split of `e` in some tree sampled from the network. `S` and `S′` are read as different splits, since otherwise the statement would fail on any tree. A set of splits is the columns of a matrix with distinct rows.
- **Galled trees and the algorithm.** [`GusfieldEtAl2004.IsReducedGalledTree`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/GalledTree.lean#L41) has the conflict graph as a parameter: [`conflictGraph`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L101) of the ancestral sequence is the source's, and [`incompatGraph`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L93) is the unrooted reading of 2007. [`GusfieldEtAl2004.Algorithm.galledBuild`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/Build.lean#L2219) is a computable function that returns a table of nodes, parents and labels, and [`GusfieldEtAl2004.Algorithm.galledBuild_correct`](../../src/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/Build.lean#L2280) says it is that of a network, which is noncomputable. Its hypotheses are the source's: there are rows, and each column has a state other than `S`'s.
- **Song and Hein's class of trees.** [`TimeOrderedTree`](../../src/Gusfield2007/TimeOrdered.lean#L24) follows line 646 of Gusfield et al., with real times and any number of children, and [`SongHein2005.IsOrdered`](../../src/External/SongHein2005/BinaryPaths.lean#L30) is the source's binary trees with distinct times. A constant column has no compatible tree in Lean, where the source calls every tree compatible with it.
- **"Efficient, counting the entry node"** ([`Gusfield2007.Network.IsEfficientAtEntry`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1721)) isn't in the paper. It is the closest reading of lines 326–328 that makes lines 341, 342 true. The idea is in Gusfield's 2014 book, whose canonical ARGs are a stricter class (E42).
- **Galled networks.** [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsGalled`](../../src/Gusfield2007/GalledNetworks.lean#L43) and [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsNonDegenerate`](../../src/Gusfield2007/GalledNetworks.lean#L47) come from what the authors published around Huson and Klöpper 2007 (Section 2), and "tree edge" there means an arc into a node that isn't a recombination node.
- **Footnote 2** speaks of "the two edges labeled with those sites" in `T̄`, whose edges carry super-characters. [`Gusfield2007.footnote2`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Figure1.lean#L188) reads it as the edges of super-characters where the site has state 1, which matches `T`'s labels and the root `00000` of Figure 1.
- **"The haplotype of one is not tight"** at line 608 is unclear, and [`Gusfield2007.fig6_line608_theorem10`](../../src/Gusfield2007/Counterexamples.lean#L357) proves it for the whole matrix and for each component.
